AI cannot fix what has never been recorded.
That is the whole AI adoption problem in one sentence, and I want to explain what I mean by it.

The strength of AI is in your data and your context, and that is exactly what many African enterprises are missing right now. It is not talent, because we have the talent. It is not the workforce either. What we are missing is the system underneath it all, and the data that system should have been collecting all along.

I am not the only one saying this. Ahead of the Digital Procurement Africa Summit in Lagos this May, Olumide "D.O" Olusanya, the CEO of Gloopro who convened the summit, put it plainly:
"AI cannot have any impact in an environment where you don't even have the data available. The first step that needs to happen is that this groundwork needs to first be built."

The groundwork he is describing is the part nobody gets excited about. Walk into a typical Nigerian business today and you will find quotations sitting inside WhatsApp chats, while the rest of the operation lives in one complex Excel sheet that only one person truly understands. If that person travels, everything slows down.

Now imagine handing that operation an AI agent. The first thing the agent needs is data, and there is simply nothing to give it.

That is why the real work right now is consolidation. Bringing your operation into one system where purchasing talks to inventory, inventory talks to payables, and every transaction leaves a record behind. If you run that way for even a year, you will have pricing history, spend patterns and delivery records. At that point AI finally has something to work with, because there is a proper foundation under it.

If you skip that step, AI or even Talents will end up taking the blame for what is really a record-keeping problem.

Build the record first. The intelligence comes after, and it comes much easier than people think.

My name is Bamidele Ajibola, founder of AlphaWGA. I have moved a 500-client oil and gas procurement firm off five scattered tools onto one system, and now I spend my time showing Nigerian businesses where their operations leak money and exactly what to fix first.
