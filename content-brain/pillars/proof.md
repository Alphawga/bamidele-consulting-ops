# Pillar: Proof (Mon story, Fri build log)

**Job:** make the Systems Audit feel inevitable. The reader should think "he has actually done this inside a business like mine."
**Reader:** Nigerian SME owner, 10 to 200 staff, procurement / supply chain / oil and gas services. Drowning in WhatsApp, Excel and one trusted staff member's memory.

## Monday format: the receipt story
Structure: moment (a real scene inside the operation) > what it was costing > what changed > the number after > one lesson.
Source material: Okoh V1 and V2 work, audits as they happen, NDI teaching moments. Ask Bamidele for fresh build notes when the well runs dry. Never invent.

## Friday format: build-in-public log
Structure: what I shipped or fixed this week > the hard decision inside it > what it taught me about business systems. Short. Honest about what broke.

## Angle bank
- The five tools Okoh retired and what replaced them
- What a quotation looks like when it lives in WhatsApp (and what it costs)
- The staff member whose head was the inventory system
- Payroll by memory: what we found and what we built
- The first week after go-live: what broke and why that was good
- Why the owner could not take a two-week holiday, and now can
- A reminder system that replaced three people chasing each other
- What 500+ clients on one system actually required
- The V2 gap list: what V1 could not do
- The report the owner had never seen before

## Hook patterns
- "N[X] was leaking every month. Nobody could see it."
- "One staff member was the inventory system."
- "The owner had not taken a holiday in [X] years."
- "We retired five tools in one quarter."
- "I shipped [thing] this week. It nearly broke me."

## CTA rule

## Quality bar
Good hook: "Five tools. One staff member's memory. Zero payroll system."
Bad hook: "Have you ever wondered how much your scattered tools are costing you?" (question hook, banned)
