# Daniel Priestley: Business Is Easy After You Achieve Product Market Fit

Raw source: `../raw/2026-07-19-daniel-priestley-business-is-easy-after-you-achieve-product-market-fit.md`.

## Core thesis

Product-market fit is the gate that precedes scaling, hiring, systems, and capital. Without it, everything is hard. With it, business becomes easy. Product-market fit happens when the ideal customer persona overlaps with an offer that packages your intellectual property into a desirable outcome.

## The entrepreneurial journey

The stages, in order:

1. **Founder opportunity fit**: soul-searching to find the right opportunity for you.
2. **Minimum viable product testing**: test concepts and audiences with forms, surveys, or waiting lists.
3. **Product-market fit**: define the ideal customer persona and the offer they respond to.
4. **Go-to-market**: crank the handle and make sales.
5. **Scale-up**: new products, markets, territories.
6. **Exit**: sell for a life-changing amount.

Then the cycle restarts. The focus here is stage 3.

## The gap that kills product-market fit

Customers have a movie in their heads: they buy a transformation, a dramatically better life, more emotional benefits. Entrepreneurs want a simple, scalable offer that does not require drama. Product-market fit exists when these two points touch. At that point:

- The customer is elated they found you and would be disappointed if you disappeared.
- You feel pressure that you cannot scale it. That is the correct feeling. Scalability comes later.

As Brian Chesky said, in the early days you do things that don't scale, then figure out scalability later.

## Step 1: Get inside the ideal customer persona's mind

Use three survey approaches:

### A. Waiting list campaign (for new offers)

Launch a landing page: "We're launching a new approach to [outcome]. Join the waiting list." Ask five questions:

1. Which best describes your current situation?
2. Which best describes your desired outcome in the next 90 days?
3. Which best describes the frustration or obstacle in your way?
4. Which best describes your budget or the solution you are most inclined to engage with?
5. Is there anything else you want us to know?

### B. Product-market fit survey (for existing customers)

Ask: "How would you feel if you could no longer use [product/service]?"

- **Very disappointed**: this is your core ideal customer persona. Follow up to understand why they rely on it, what outcome they achieve, and what they value most.
- **Somewhat disappointed**: ask what features or services would tip them into "very disappointed."
- **Okay with it**: ignore them for product-market fit purposes; they are not your market.

### C. Experience score

Ask: "How would you rate your experience with us today?" Then ask which best describes them, their desired outcome, their frustration, and what would improve the score by at least one point.

## Step 2: Radically improve the offer

Create **gold, silver, and bronze** versions of the offer. Each must be profitable.

- **Gold**: over-the-top, includes every valuable component you can deliver.
- **Bronze**: the basics that still achieve the outcome.
- **Silver**: middle option with a few frills for faster results.

Use AI to analyze survey data and draft the offer packages. Then get a designer to produce a brochure, landing page, and slide deck. Remember: 50% of the brain is visual processing. People cannot get excited about what they cannot see. Multi-million dollar properties sell off-plan with a brochure and a model; your offer needs visual aids too.

## Step 3: 30 one-to-one sales meetings

Present the new gold, silver, and bronze offer to at least 30 prospects. The goal is to get at least three commitments to silver or gold.

## Step 4: The 90-day final test

After three customers have experienced the new offering for 90 days, ask:

1. Would you be disappointed if we took this away?
2. How would you rate your experience (1 to 10)?
3. Would you enthusiastically recommend this to someone like you?

If the answers are "yes," 9-10, and "yes," you have product-market fit.

## The emotional barrier

Most founders feel embarrassed asking customers what they want. They think it looks amateur. The opposite is true: asking is the professional move. Customers love being asked and love being shown new offers. The biggest trap is being "good but not great" — lukewarm customers who keep buying but are not raving fans. That is almost product-market fit, but not quite. Heroic sales and marketing can paper over the gap, but the goal is to make signing up effortless because the fit is so strong.

## How this maps to AlphaWGA

- **Current stage**: AlphaWGA is likely between MVP testing and product-market fit. The offer ladder exists, but the ideal customer persona and the exact offer mix may not be tightly validated yet.
- **Waiting list campaign**: the scorecard can be upgraded to ask Priestley's five questions. This is the fastest way to gather data on the current ideal customer persona.
- **Product-market fit survey**: once AlphaWGA has even a small customer base, run the "How would you feel if you could no longer use..." survey.
- **Experience score**: add a one-question post-diagnostic or post-audit rating, followed by the segmentation questions.
- **Gold, silver, bronze**: the offer ladder (scorecard → diagnostic → audit → blueprint → advisor) is close, but it may need to be explicitly packaged as gold/silver/bronze versions of the core transformation, not just sequential steps. The current ladder is process-based; product-market fit may require outcome-based tiers.
- **Visual aids**: the audit/blueprint/advisor offers need brochures and slide decks. Without them, prospects cannot see the value.
- **30 meetings**: set a target of 30 one-to-one sales meetings presenting the refined offer. This is the validation loop.
- **90-day test**: after three advisor/audit clients complete 90 days, ask the three product-market fit questions.
- **Pricing**: if the fit is strong, price objections melt away. This is a signal to test, not a reason to underprice.
