# Daniel Priestley: Key Person of Influence — distilled notes

Raw source: `../raw/Key-Person-of-Influence-Revised-Edition.pdf`. Full book read via chunked
extraction (200 pages). This note distills the five-step KPI method plus the execution
psychology in Part 3.

## Core thesis

Every industry has an inner circle of "Key People of Influence" (KPIs): well-known,
well-connected, highly valued people who curate opportunities instead of chasing them. This
status is not a function of decades in an industry, degrees, or a wealthy start. It comes
from a five-step method, done in order, over roughly 12 months.

Why the old rules broke: the Industrial Age rewarded functional competence (doing a job
well); the current economy is digital, global, intangible, and entrepreneurial, and rewards
**vital** people over **functional** ones. A functional person performs a replaceable
process. A vital person redefines the game and is irreplaceable. "A functional person is
worn out by their functionality; a vital person is re-energised by their vitality."

Every industry is fragmenting into micro-niches, and each micro-niche needs its own KPI. The
internet lets small, specific, trustworthy operators out-compete big generalist companies.
Settlers who stake a claim to a niche win; swagmen who stay undecided lose. The hidden theme
the book never states outright: you already possess the value, the five steps are packaging
and communication, not reinvention. "You are already standing on a mountain of value."

## The five steps (in required order)

Order matters: you can't productize a pitch that doesn't exist yet; you can't build profile
on nothing published; partnerships don't arrive until people already know you're a KPI.

### 1. Pitch

The ability to answer "What do you do?" so it provokes an emotional reaction (love it, hate
it, act on it) rather than a **polite response** ("sounds interesting," a nod) — the polite
response is the worst outcome because it signals disengagement, not disagreement.

- **Big Game exercise**: define the 3-year mission with fun, rules, players, a prize, a way
  to win, a way to lose.
- Pick a micro-niche: sniper rifle, not shotgun.
- Rebuild the generic answer into a specific, benefit-loaded one, grounded in a Customer
  Insight, Technology Insight, or Industry Insight.
- 7-step presentation pitch: position yourself as credible → name the problem and how you
  noticed it → extrapolate its impact → solve it → prove it (stats/case study/endorsement) →
  ask for what you want → leave people uplifted.
- Three core payoffs for the listener, not features about yourself. Treat "that won't work"
  as a request for more information.

### 2. Publish

Published content is the title deed to your niche in the ideas economy. "It's no coincidence
that the word 'authority' has the word 'author' in it." Writing also sharpens your own
thinking and surfaces IP you didn't know you had.

Five book types, pick one: niche thought-leadership, book of interviews (features other
KPIs, builds network more than personal authority), book of tips (10 categories x 10 tips),
picture book (visual industries), creative/parable piece. Target 30,000-50,000 words, or
repurpose into 10 articles (1,000-1,500 words) if a full book isn't feasible yet. Most books
sell under 500 copies and that's fine — the book's job is to promote you, not itself.

### 3. Product

"Money moves towards hot products," but single products don't make money on their own —
**product ecosystems** do: a mix of free, low-cost, and high-value offers working together.
In an ad-blind world, the product is the marketing.

Two product types to build:
- **High-volume products** (relationship-building): free podcast, info kit, webinar series.
- **High-value products** (profit-driving): implementation, not information — "the value of
  getting something done for you has risen" as information has fallen in value.

Productized services: give the service a name, a method, standardized plus bespoke elements.
Business valuation heuristic: **Value = Profit x Multiple** — owned IP/products command a
higher multiple than resold services or pure time-for-money delivery.

### 4. Profile

"You are who Google says you are." A KPI must be findable, coherent, and credible on search,
not necessarily famous. Six objectives profile-building should serve: generate leads,
convert them, increase average spend, increase purchase frequency, reduce costs (marketing,
comms, service), improve overall brand. "Nothing is more disappointing than meeting someone
who seems interesting, but who doesn't come up in a Google search."

### 5. Partnerships

"The money step." Real wealth compounds by leveraging what other KPIs already built (their
list, brand, distribution, team) instead of building everything from zero. "Someone has
already built a relationship with thousands of people who could be your clients. That list
is on someone's hard drive right now."

Deal types to start with: affiliate partnerships, co-promotion (two similar-sized lists
cross-promote), product-creation partnerships (joint new offer), packaging (bundle with a
complementary product), free-bundle groups (shared lead magnet).

Execution rule: never ask "how do I make it happen," ask "who do I need to talk to" — the
three-calls exercise. Meeting protocol: neutral/nice venue, small thoughtful gift, friendship
before business, business capped at 15-20 minutes, follow through within 10-15 minutes of
anything promised.

## Part 3: making it happen (the execution psychology)

- **Three industry layers**: Newcomers (enthusiastic, work for free) → Worker-bees (doing
  the work, going nowhere, resentful of both newcomers and KPIs) → KPIs (opportunities flow
  to them). The "merry-go-round of distraction": worker-bees keep switching niches hoping for
  an easy win, and reset to zero each time. The fix is finishing all five steps in one niche
  before considering another.
- **Illusion of Limited Resources (ILR)**: money, people, and opportunity aren't actually
  scarce — resourcefulness is. Three levers: the questions you ask, the people you know, your
  willingness to stretch into the unknown.
- **"Resourcefulness shows up after you make a commitment, not before."** Don't wait for
  spare time or perfect conditions — commit to the deadline first, then get resourceful.
- **Law of Inertia vs. Law of Momentum**: act on a spark immediately; delay lets inertia win,
  and the "outside force" that eventually breaks inertia is usually pain, not choice.
- Once all five steps are in place: publicity requests, speaking invitations, board seats,
  membership groups, paid communities, unsolicited VIP treatment.

## Diagnostic tools in the book

- **KPI Scorecard** (external, keypersonofinfluence.com/scorecard): scores 0-10 per step,
  8-10 = high, 4-7 = moderate, 1-4 = low per area; overall >80% high, 45-80% moderate, <45%
  low. Includes yes/no diagnostics like "If someone Googled your name, would most of page 1
  be positive links featuring you?"
- **Five qualifying questions** Priestley mentally runs on people at live events: What do you
  do? What makes you credible? How do you make your money? Are you known/liked/trusted by
  others? Who or what else can you bring to a deal?
