# Daniel Priestley: 25 Years of Sales Knowledge in 34 Minutes

Raw source: `../raw/2026-07-19-daniel-priestley-25-years-of-sales-knowledge-in-34-minutes.md`.

## Core thesis

Sales is a system, not a talent. The system is **LAPS**: **L**eads, **A**ppointments, **P**resentations, **S**ales. Get a consistent weekly LAPS rhythm and the business grows predictably. Most people fail because they skip the lead and appointment work, present poorly, or neglect follow-up.

## Leads: attention → trust → signal of interest

People need to see you 11 times within 90 days before they notice you. Post daily on social media; small businesses should start with one platform, then expand to four as they grow. The clock resets every 90 days.

The lead-generation chain is:

- **Short-form content** (1 to 3 minutes): posts, images, short videos, carousels. Hook attention, point to long-form content.
- **Long-form content** (10 minutes to 2 hours): long posts, PDF reports, 10-minute videos, podcasts, workshops. People need 2 to 7 hours with you to know and trust you. Build up to four pieces of long-form content over 12 months.
- **Signal of interest**: the prospect moves from social media to your landing page. Strong signals: waiting list, form, assessment, discussion group, live webinar registration.

To accelerate: boost top-performing short-form content with ads, send direct messages to selected individuals, and use joint ventures or sponsorships with non-competitive audiences.

## Appointments: from lead to committed time

The first sale is not money; it is a commitment of time. When a lead comes in, follow up fast. The goal is to book an appointment.

Four steps to book:

1. **Reaffirm relevance**: remind them they filled in a form and why you are calling.
2. **Ask permission** (for phone): "Have I caught you at a good time?"
3. **Pitch value**: the hook pitch using **name, same, fame, pain, aim, game**.
   - **Name**: who you are and where you are from.
   - **Same**: category they already understand.
   - **Fame**: what makes you special (numbers, awards, big-brand clients).
   - **Pain**: the frustration you address.
   - **Aim**: what you aim to do about the pain.
   - **Game**: the bigger vision.
4. **Ask for the appointment**: offer a booking link or ask for times.

Expect less than half of leads to book an appointment. That is normal.

Advanced: add lead qualification questions on the signal-of-interest form (current situation, desired outcome, solutions already in mind). In the early days, take any appointment to hone skills; later, qualify to avoid wasting time.

## The sales presentation: 10 components

1. **Framing**: show up high-value before you speak. Venue, background, pre-meeting communication, and professionalism all set the frame.
2. **Rapport**: friendly chitchat. Use their name, find common interests, smile, be polite, be complimentary.
3. **Permission to proceed**: ask whether they want a chitchat or a structured process. Most choose the process.
4. **Present situation**: how they describe where they are now and what is less than perfect.
5. **Desired outcome / prize**: the movie they play in their mind about life with the result. People buy the prize, not the product.
6. **Problem / obstacles**: what has stopped them from getting the prize, or what criteria matter to them.

Listen more than talk while filling these three baskets. Only then shift to speaking.

7. **Insights**: a big-picture perspective or framework that shows you understand their world better than they do. Has nothing to do with your product yet.
8. **Method**: the methodology that leads from the insight to the solution.
9. **Solution**: present gold, silver, and bronze options. Use visual aids (slide deck or brochure). The brain is more than 50% visual; if people cannot see it, they will not buy it.
10. **Discussion**: ask "How does that land for you?" and shut up. Let them think. Do not play conversational tennis. Gather all objections before responding, then answer them as one grouped response.

**Completing**: if they are ready, close the paperwork and onboard. If not, get a concrete next step in the diary immediately.

## The insights golf bag

Prepare 5 to 10 insights and methods in advance. Each is a "club" for a specific situation (a particular prize, a common problem, a certain present situation). Rehearse them so you can pull out the right one at the right time.

## Sales follow-up: where money is doubled

The first 48 hours after a presentation is the peak closing window. After that, follow up at least seven times over one week to three months. Reasons to follow up: special offers, new insights, fresh testimonials, or specific events discussed in the presentation.

If seven follow-ups do not close the deal, move to a **nurture sequence**: friendly social media or email touchpoints (testimonials, insights, special offers, news commentary, "Are you still interested in X?").

Eventually run a **reactivation campaign**: send a direct email — "Have you given up on [outcome]?" If they are still interested, send them an assessment or calculator to re-engage and restart the LAPS process.

## Speed and confidence

- **Speed**: follow up within a minute if possible. "I was just about to call someone and your name popped up." This is not dating; do not play it cool.
- **Confidence**: comes from repetition and consistency. Keep a weekly LAPS dashboard and aim for steady numbers (e.g., 50 leads → 10 appointments → 6 presentations → 2 sales). Consistency beats spikes.

## Using AI in sales

- Record every sales presentation with an AI note-taker.
- Transcribe them and feed them into AI.
- Ask AI to: build a better golf bag of insights and methods, improve the offer, and identify common themes between meetings that won vs. meetings that bombed.

## How this maps to AlphaWGA

- **LAPS**: the current offer ladder needs a visible LAPS dashboard. Define the weekly target for leads, diagnostic appointments, audit presentations, and advisor closes.
- **Lead generation**: the scorecard is the signal-of-interest landing page. The content pipeline (LinkedIn/YouTube) is the short-form and long-form content engine. This confirms that daily posting and regular long-form content are not optional.
- **11 times in 90 days**: AlphaWGA should aim for daily presence on at least one platform to start, then expand to four.
- **Appointment pitch**: the hook pitch (name, same, fame, pain, aim, game) should be scripted for the diagnostic call follow-up.
- **Presentation structure**: the diagnostic and audit calls should follow the 10-component structure. The current offer ladder is the solution; the missing pieces are the explicit framing, the three baskets (present situation, desired outcome, obstacles), and the insights/method before the solution.
- **Visual aids**: the audit and advisor offers need slide decks or brochures showing gold/silver/bronze. The current offer playbook is text-based; visual versions will lift conversion.
- **Follow-up**: after every diagnostic and audit, there must be a 48-hour follow-up and a seven-touch sequence. This is currently underdefined.
- **AI**: record and transcribe sales calls (with permission) to build the insights golf bag. This is especially important in the early stage where patterns are still being discovered.
- **Speed**: leads from the scorecard should be followed up within minutes, not days.
