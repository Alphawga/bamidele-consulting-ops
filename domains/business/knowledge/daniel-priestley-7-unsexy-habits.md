# Daniel Priestley: 7 Unsexy Habits That Build a $1 Million Business

Raw source: `../raw/2026-07-19-daniel-priestley-7-unsexy-habits-that-build-a-1-million-business.md`.

## Core thesis

Seven-figure businesses are built from boring, repeatable habits, not motivational morning routines. Ninety percent of activity does not matter; ten percent is critical. Get the critical habits right and repeat them.

## The seven habits

### 1. Design a perfect repeatable week

A million-dollar year is roughly $25,000 per week for 40 weeks. Map out the entire year as a repeating $25,000 week. Create a future-state org chart and document, hour by hour, what each team member does in that perfect week. Include ads, content, lead follow-up, sales calls, team meetings, and the P&L for the week. The goal is a machine that repeats with minimal variation. Do not make it up week by week.

### 2. Enroll a team

Business is a team sport. You cannot run onto the field alone and recruit players after you score. Assemble 4 to 8 people before launch or before scaling. The entrepreneur's job is to enroll people: friends, network contacts, cold outreach, even people met in random places. Most will say no; enough will say yes. If you are not willing to recruit a team, you may not be ready to lead; in that case, join someone else's team until you are.

### 3. Run a quarterly planning retreat

Spend one full day every quarter with the team to plan the next 90 days. Focus heavily on marketing materials and offer documents: ads, content, landing pages, explainer videos, slide decks, brochures, and the offer document ("this is what you get and this is how much it is"). The easiest path to $25,000 weeks is selling a package between $2,000 and $10,000, requiring only a few sales per week. Everyone should leave the retreat knowing exactly what they must produce, hour by hour and week by week, for the next 90 days.

### 4. Generate leads relentlessly

A business exists because someone wants to buy, not because you have something to sell. Leads are people who signal interest in writing: forms, assessments, emails, DMs, comments. To make five sales a week, you may need 150 leads a week. Generate them through organic content on three or four platforms, ads, cold DMs, and outreach. Priestley's benchmark: a low-wage hire should do 70 to 100 cold outreaches per day. Founders should expect to hustle at that level to get off the ground.

### 5. Bookend the week with Monday and Friday meetings

- **Monday morning meeting** (30 to 60 minutes): the whole team agrees on the 3 to 6 critical things the company must achieve that week, and each person commits to their own 3 to 6 things. The meeting should have energy and creative conflict. Priestley calls this "locking antlers": deliberately arguing about what will create the biggest impact.
- **Friday afternoon debrief**: reflect on what could have been better, what was forgotten, what was left on the table, who performed well, and what to carry forward.

The founder must lead both meetings. Do not delegate them.

### 6. Run a weekly introduction workshop

The introduction workshop introduces prospects to what you do, why it is valuable, how much it costs, and what they get. Run it live every week, preferably on Zoom, as a fixed fixture. Do not record and automate it until you have delivered it live at least 50 times. Repetition makes the value proposition sharper, funnier, and more engaging.

### 7. Environment dictates performance

Spend time every week with people who are 5 to 10 times bigger than you. They should be on the same journey but further ahead, so their mindset is reachable but stretching. Sources: mentors, coaches, suppliers, accelerator programs, peer groups. Also surround yourself with best practices (e.g., educational content), accountability (someone who counts your results with you), and access to resources (investors, tech help, hiring leads). Avoid mentors who are 100x bigger; their context is too far removed to be useful.

## How this maps to AlphaWGA

- **Perfect repeatable week**: the offer ladder and content calendar exist, but the hour-by-hour operating rhythm is not yet documented. This is the next operational layer to define.
- **$25,000 week / $2-10K offer**: the advisor/audit offers sit in this price range. The math is clear: 3 to 4 sales per week at $8K hits the target. This validates the pricing strategy.
- **Team**: Bamidele is currently solo. The habit of enrolling a team is the single biggest blocker between present state and seven figures. The first hires should be mapped in the future-state org chart.
- **Quarterly retreat**: set a recurring 90-day planning event. The first retreat should produce the marketing materials and offer documents for the next quarter.
- **Lead generation velocity**: the current plan is heavy on content but light on outbound volume. Priestley's benchmark of 70 to 100 outreaches per day and 150 leads per week for 5 sales is a concrete standard to measure against.
- **Introduction workshop**: the diagnostic call or a group audit presentation could become a weekly intro workshop. The format should be delivered live repeatedly before any automation.
- **Monday/Friday meetings**: even as a solo founder, Bamidele can run a personal Monday/Friday review. As the team grows, this becomes the company operating rhythm.
- **Environment**: Dent Global or a similar accelerator/peer group should be evaluated. The right environment provides mentors, accountability, and resources.
