# AlphaWGA voice cheat sheet

This is the voice that already shows up in Bamidele's best writing. It is not invented here. It is extracted from the 2026-07-17 LinkedIn post, the draft files, and the backlog seeds. Use it as the filter for every piece of content.

## What the voice is

- **Direct.** Short sentences. One idea per line. No throat-clearing.
- **Concrete.** Nigerian businesses, Lagos, oil and gas, WhatsApp quotes, Excel sheets, one person who knows the system. The reader should see a specific scene, not a general concept.
- **Self-aware.** Willing to admit his own mistakes. "I was not doing the work." "I have one post published, ever."
- **Contrarian without being clickbait.** Willing to say the thing that is true but unpopular: input feels like progress, AI cannot fix missing records, the polite nod is failure.
- **Evidence-based.** Uses real names, real numbers, real client stories. When numbers are missing, he marks `[TO FILL]` rather than guessing.
- **Confident, not salesy.** The proof does the selling. No question hooks. No jargon. No "unlock your potential."
- **Nigerian context.** Naira, local tools, local constraints, local opportunities. Not generic global business advice with a Nigerian name swapped in.

## What the voice is not

- Not academic. Do not write paragraphs with multiple clauses.
- Not motivational. Do not tell people to "leverage" or "streamline" or "unlock."
- Not generic AI slop. Do not use phrases like "in today's fast-paced world" or "the power of AI."
- Not humble-brag. If it is about him, it is about a specific mistake or a specific lesson, not a general virtue.

## Sentence patterns

- Open with a single sentence that lands a fact: "AI cannot fix what has never been recorded."
- Use short paragraphs. One to three sentences each.
- Explain the concept through a specific business scene: "Walk into a typical Nigerian business today and you will find quotations sitting inside WhatsApp chats..."
- Quote real people: "Olumide 'D.O' Olusanya... put it plainly: 'AI cannot have any impact...'"
- End with a clear takeaway or a challenge: "Build the record first."
- Sign off with a one-line bio only when necessary, and keep it factual: "My name is Bamidele Ajibola, founder of AlphaWGA. I have moved a 500-client oil and gas procurement firm off five scattered tools onto one system."

## Opening styles that work

1. **The single fact:** "AI cannot fix what has never been recorded."
2. **The contradiction:** "Input feels like progress. It isn't."
3. **The scene:** "One staff member was the inventory system."
4. **The honest number:** "I have one LinkedIn post published, ever. I have fifteen ideas sitting in a backlog file."
5. **The rule:** "The polite response is the worst outcome."

## CTA style

- Optional P.S. line only.
- Direct and low-pressure: "P.S. I find these leaks for a living. DM me SCORECARD for the free self-audit."
- No long pitch. No button language. No urgency tricks.

## How to write a post in this voice

1. Pick one angle from the calendar.
2. Choose an opening style from the list above.
3. Write one concrete scene or example from your own experience or a client's.
4. State the takeaway in one or two lines.
5. Add the P.S. only if the post supports a next step.
6. Read it aloud. If it sounds like a LinkedIn influencer, rewrite it.

## Red flags to cut

- Any sentence that could appear in 100 other posts.
- Phrases: "in today's world," "the future of," "leverage," "unlock," "seamless," "synergy," "streamline."
- Paragraphs longer than four lines on a phone screen.
- Question hooks.
- Claims without evidence.
- Em dashes. Use periods, commas, colons, or brackets instead.

## How to keep improving the voice

- After every post, save the best-performing version in `domains/business/knowledge/published/`.
- Once a month, review the last 10 published posts and update this cheat sheet with any new patterns.
- If a post feels flat, compare it to the 2026-07-17 post and identify what is missing: concrete scene, specific name, Nigerian context, or a clear takeaway.
