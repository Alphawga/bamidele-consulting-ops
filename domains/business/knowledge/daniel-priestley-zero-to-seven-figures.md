# Daniel Priestley: Give Me 29 Minutes and I’ll Teach You to Make $1 Million

Raw source: `../raw/2026-07-19-daniel-priestley-give-me-29-minutes-and-ill-teach-you-to-make-1-million.md`.

## Core thesis

Going from zero to seven figures is not a single leap. It is a sequence of five stages, split into "below the line" preparation and "above the line" company-building. Each stage has a different goal, team size, product mix, and cadence. Skip the preparation and you launch without commercial awareness, self-awareness, or resources. Skip the stages and you try to scale a team before you have product-market fit.

## The five stages

### 1. Apprenticeship (below the line)

Spend 1 to 2 years working for an experienced entrepreneur. The purpose is not to earn a salary; it is to collect three things:

- **Commercial awareness**: how businesses make sales, look after customers, manage cash, and operate day-to-day.
- **Self-awareness**: what you are good at, weak at, and how you work under pressure.
- **Access to resources**: how to get money, run ads, hire, and use tools.

Do not skip this by declaring it impossible. If you cannot find an apprenticeship, the goal is still to acquire these three assets through any available route.

### 2. Side hustle (below the line)

Run 90-day, open-and-shut side hustles. The goal is not revenue. It is to test your entrepreneurial skills and build confidence without getting stuck. These are small, low-risk campaigns that end cleanly so you can move on.

### 3. Startup / chaos (above the line)

This is where you become a fully fledged entrepreneur. Priestley frames the work as **CHAOS**: **C**oncept, **A**udience, **O**ffer, **S**ales.

- Work in a **two-person scout team** (co-founder, mentor, early employee, or assistant). The two of you refine the four variables together.
- Run **MVPs**, especially waiting-list landing pages. A waiting list should ask five questions: which best describes you, which result are you trying to achieve, what is your biggest challenge, what else have you tried, and what is your current budget.
- Do **30 to 150 one-to-one sales meetings**. Treat rejection as free consulting. The goal is product-market fit, not revenue. Revenue at this stage is typically 10K to 100K as a signal, not a target.
- Look for **founder-opportunity fit**: an overlap between a pain you removed, a task you were paid for, and a topic you are passionate about.
- Keep tools free (Google/Apple/Microsoft suites, spreadsheets). Do not automate too early; do it by hand first so you understand the flow.
- Run a **Monday morning meeting** to set 3 to 6 high-value weekly tasks, and a **Friday debrief** to check them off.

### 4. Scale-up

The business becomes a lifestyle business with revenue, fun, freedom, and flexibility.

- **Team of four**: an associate key person of influence (lends name/reputation), sales and marketing, customer success/delivery, and operations/social media.
- **Two products**: a **product for prospects** (intro workshop, assessment) and a **core offer** (gold, silver, bronze). Promote the product for prospects, not the core offer. The product for prospects leads to a one-to-one sales meeting, which leads to the core offer.
- **Perfect repeatable week**: a weekly rhythm that generates 50K to 100K per month. Ingredients include small-budget ads with an allowable cost per sale, daily social posts on at least three platforms, 100 DMs or emails per day, one-to-one selling, group presentations, team meetings, and customer feedback sessions.
- **Joint ventures**: distribution partners, complementary product partners, and brand partners. Also build a **squad** of 10 non-competing small businesses in a WhatsApp group to engage with each other's social posts and feed the algorithms.

### 5. Seven-figure business / authority

The founder becomes the **key person of influence**. The business runs as a tight ship with formalized systems.

- **Team of 8 to 12**: general manager, head of marketing, salesperson, appointment setter, head of product/delivery, head of customer success, head of IT/automation, assistant, social media marketer. Do not go far above 12 without splitting the organization.
- **Four product types**: free **gift**, **product for prospects**, **core offer** (gold/silver/bronze), and **product for clients** (subscription or continuation offer).
- **Three-part year**: a perfect repeatable week, 3 to 4 spotlight campaigns every 3 to 4 months, and one annual big message that you repeat across 100 pieces of content.
- **Tools**: CRM, AI automations, customer/team portal, Slack or Teams.
- **SAND dashboard**: the metrics you need to sleep at night knowing you are on track for seven figures.
- **Revenue math**: average at least 20K to 25K per week, which is 100K+ per month and 1M+ per year. That might be 10 sales at 10K, 20 at 5K, or 100 at 1K.

## How this maps to AlphaWGA

- **Apprenticeship / side hustle**: Bamidele's background as a technical architect and operator is the equivalent foundation. The gap to close is not commercial awareness but packaging that awareness into repeatable sales and marketing systems.
- **CHAOS / MVP**: the waiting-list framework is directly applicable. The Operations Self-Audit Scorecard and the diagnostic call in the offer ladder already function as a product for prospects. The five waiting-list questions can be adapted to the scorecard intake form to gather better data.
- **30 to 150 sales meetings**: this is the missing operating metric for the early stage. The current playbook names the offer ladder but does not yet set a hard target for discovery calls before product-market fit is declared.
- **Perfect repeatable week**: the content calendar and weekly rhythm in the operator guide should be explicitly tied to revenue math. Priestley's 100 DMs/emails per day and daily social posts on three platforms set a concrete bar for outbound activity.
- **Product for prospects**: this validates the existing offer ladder. The free scorecard is the gift/intro product; the diagnostic is the low-commitment product for prospects; the audit/blueprint/advisor rungs are the core offer in gold/silver/bronze form. The language is already aligned.
- **Squad / JV partners**: this is underdeveloped in the current playbook. A WhatsApp squad of non-competing operators could amplify the LinkedIn and YouTube content without paid ads.
- **Annual big message**: the core line "I consolidate scattered business operations into one intelligent system" is the annual big message. The content pillars should be checked against this to ensure every piece reinforces it.
- **SAND dashboard**: the offer playbook should define the leading indicators that prove the system is on track to seven figures, not just lagging revenue numbers.
