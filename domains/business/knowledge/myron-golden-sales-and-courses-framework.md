# Myron Golden: Low-pressure selling and course creation — distilled notes

## Low-pressure, high-ticket selling

### The right mindset

- **Selling is the common denominator of every business.** Doctors sell wellness, lawyers sell advocacy, developers sell outcomes. If you want to own a business, you must learn to sell.
- **Scarcity creates commission breath.** When you need the sale, the buyer smells it. Operate from abundance: there are already thousands of people who would love to buy what you sell if they only knew you existed.
- **People love to buy and love to be sold, but they hate to be convinced.** Convincing is getting someone to do what you want for your reasons. Selling is helping them do what they already want for their reasons.
- **Detach from the outcome.** Your energy should communicate, "Buy it or don't buy it. I'm good either way." That removes pressure from both sides.
- **No competition.** If a prospect is comparing you to someone else, tell them to buy the other offer. You only want people who are sure your solution is the best fit.

### The consultant salesperson

- Ask questions about their pain and pleasure. Let them tell you enough for you to give the exact information they need to say yes.
- Don't sell like a dentist who prescribes a root canal before looking at the teeth. Diagnose first, then prescribe.
- The prescription is **not** the pieces (workbooks, modules), **not** the process, and **not** your personal time. The prescription is the **payoff**: what they can do after that they couldn't do before.

### Making buyers find you

- **Stop hunting buyers. Make yourself more findable.** Algorithms on LinkedIn, YouTube, Instagram, etc., are better at finding clients than you are.
- **Audience building is upstream from lead generation.** More audience → more leads → more sales.
- **Community service content:** content so valuable you could have sold it. Give it away to prove you can help. Rule of thumb: at least 10 service pieces for every 1 promotional piece.
- If you're worried that giving content away leaves nothing to sell, your well isn't deep enough.

### Who and how to help

- **Who are you going to help?** Be specific. "I help indigenous Nigerian procurement and oil-and-gas service firms replace scattered tools with one operating system."
- **Help them the way they want to be helped.** Solve the problem they know they have before the problem you know they have.
- **Business owners are the easiest buyers.** They already spend money to stay in business. They put critical expenses on autopay.
- **Sell to people who want it, not just people who need it.** Rich people like to make more money. Broke people often suspect opportunity.

### Where value comes from

- **Past perceived voids** create present pursued values. (What have they been missing?)
- **Present perceived virtues** create present pursued values. (What do they already see as good?)
- **Future perceived visions** create present pursued values. (What do they want to become?)
- Find an underserved, undervalued market, develop a solution, deploy the value. The demand is already there; you are uncovering it.

### Language shift

- Stop saying "high-ticket offer." That focuses on what you get. Say **premium value offer**, which focuses on what the client gets.
- The transformation must be **measurable, desirable, believable, and statable in a sound bite**.

## Courses, coaching, and consulting that create cash

### Why coaching/course businesses are necessary now

- The world is changing faster than ever (AI age). People need guides to navigate the new landscape.
- Economic eras: land → machines → outlets → tech know-how → information control → techno-info-entertainment → partnership platforms → AI.
- In the AI age, the people who win are those who can guide others through rapid change.

### Why most courses and coaching fail

1. **Guesswork.** The creator succeeded once but doesn't know why. They can't reproduce it for others.
2. **Scammers.** They don't care if the buyer gets results.
3. **Overwhelming / not distilled.** They think more content = more value. More is not better. **Faster is better.** The sooner the buyer gets the payoff, the longer they enjoy it.

### What people are actually buying

- They are not buying videos, workbooks, modules, or apps. They are buying the **transformation**.
- Stop selling trinkets. Start selling the transformation.

### The six phases of transformation

1. **Awareness.** The buyer becomes aware that a desirable result is possible and that someone can help them get it.
2. **Intention.** They decide, "I am going to produce this result."
3. **Disruption.** Pain, difficulty, and struggle always follow intention. The buyer must be prepared to push through.
4. **Decision.** A true decision cuts off all other options. "I will do this or die in the doing." Not a choice you can put back on the shelf.
5. **Discipline.** Do what you're supposed to do, the way you're supposed to do it, when you're supposed to do it, every time.
6. **Recognition.** Notice how far you've come. Then **celebration** with milestones and family.

A good course or coaching program takes people through all six phases.

## How this applies to AlphaWGA

### Selling

- Bamidele's natural mode is to explain the process (the ERP, the tech stack, the integrations). Myron says: stop selling the pieces and the process. Sell the payoff: "You will know exactly where every order, payment, and delivery stands without chasing staff on WhatsApp."
- The paid diagnostic call is the perfect consultative sales tool. It diagnoses the problem they know they have before prescribing anything.
- Stop chasing prospects. Build content that makes you findable to Nigerian procurement and oil-and-gas operators who are already looking for operational consolidation.
- The Okoh ERP is the social proof. One ounce of proof is worth more than 10,000 pounds of promise.
- Target business owners, not budget-conscious startups. They already spend to stay in business.
- Use the language of premium value, not high-ticket. The consolidation thesis is already measurable, desirable, believable, and statable.

### Course creation

- The AI automation course should not be a dump of AI tools. It should be a transformation: "Take your operation from scattered to consolidated using AI + systems."
- Anchor every module to real results. Okoh is the case study.
- Distill ruthlessly. The buyer needs the fastest path to the first win, not every possible tool.
- Design the curriculum around the six phases: make them aware of what's possible, set intention, warn them about disruption, force a decision, build daily discipline, and celebrate milestones.
- If the course can't yet be reproduced for others, don't launch it yet. Document and systematize the Okoh result first.
