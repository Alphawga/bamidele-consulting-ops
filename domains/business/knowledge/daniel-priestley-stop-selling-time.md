# Daniel Priestley: Why You Must Stop Selling Your Time for Money

Raw source: `../raw/2026-07-19-daniel-priestley-why-you-must-stop-selling-your-time-for-money.md`.

## Core thesis

The time-for-money model is being undermined by token economics. AI can replace a year of knowledge-worker labor for roughly $200,000, and that cost is falling. If you invoice by the hour, day, or monthly retainer, you are pricing yourself against a force that is cheaper and faster. The only durable move is to stop selling time and start selling outcomes, systems, and products.

## Two hard problems every business must solve

1. **Speed to value**: the ability to deliver outcomes for customers. Customers do not want your process; they want a result. They would wave a magic wand if they could. Your job is to be the fastest, cheapest way to that result.
2. **Speed to market**: the ability to tell your story and let people know you can deliver the outcome. A single appearance on the right podcast can explode a business because it massively increases speed to market.

Everything else is noise.

## AI as digital soil

In the agricultural age, soil automated most of the value creation (steps 3 to 8): plant the seed, and the land grows the wheat. AI is the digital soil of the current economy. The human's job is now:

- Step 1: know what to do
- Step 2: set up the technology and prompt it
- Steps 3 to 8: AI executes the work
- Step 9: know when it is done
- Step 10: take it to market and sell it

The execution layer is commoditizing. The decision layer is where value lives.

## Five things you actually get paid for now

1. **Conviction**: the life-force energy behind wanting to see something happen in the world. AI has no conviction. Conviction demands action; without action, it leaves you and finds someone else.
2. **Judgment**: making good choices, setting priorities, picking paths, choosing tools, assembling teams. Judgment comes from experience, and experience comes from bad judgment.
3. **Taste**: creative choices that align with the ideal customer persona. Taste comes from spending time with your market and resonating with their energy.
4. **Commercial outcomes**: making sales and delivering results. This is speed to market and speed to value in practice. If you can claim ownership of a result, you get paid for it.
5. **Improving the system**: staying up to date with tools, refining the system, asking how to go faster and produce better data.

Reframe your role from task-doer to decision-maker. The old economy paid for execution; the new economy pays for the five things above.

## Stop fighting AI, surf it

Do not wish the genie back in the bottle. AI disrupts every industry, but that disruption also unlocks new value. The opportunity is to be downstream from the disruption — or to be the disruption yourself. Every inefficiency in the economy will be reallocated to new, better solutions. If you can provide one of those solutions, you can capture that value.

## The path out: productize your expertise

Priestley's example: an IT agency building assessments on WordPress for $15,000 to $20,000 each became ScoreApp, a subscription platform with tens of thousands of customers. The real value was not the coding; it was the conviction, judgment, and taste about the strategy. The same thinking turned a publishing company into bookmagic.ai and a video production company into a video platform.

The result: a scalable business where you do not sell time for money. You can serve thousands of customers without your time being the bottleneck.

## The new role: key person of influence

In a post-AI world, the founder's job is to be the key person of influence for the business: known, liked, trusted, and respected for a specific outcome. It is not about being an influencer. It is about being the person whose name comes up in conversation when people need that outcome.

## How this maps to AlphaWGA

- **The business model threat**: AlphaWGA currently sells advisory and implementation services. If the value is delivered through hours of Bamidele's time, the model is exposed to AI commoditization. The advisory layer is valuable, but the implementation layer will be increasingly automated.
- **Speed to value**: the outcome is not "I built you a system." The outcome is "your operations are consolidated, your decisions are faster, your cash flow is clearer, your team is less scattered." Every offer should be framed around the outcome, not the hours.
- **Speed to market**: the content pipeline and the scorecard are the speed-to-market engine. The core line and the pillars must be repeated until the market associates Bamidele with the outcome.
- **Digital soil**: AI should automate steps 3 to 8 of service delivery wherever possible. Documentation, reporting, workflow automation, and data consolidation are all candidates. Bamidele's role becomes conviction, judgment, taste, commercial outcomes, and improving the system.
- **Productization**: the tool-ideas backlog is the right place to capture reusable pieces. The long-term move is to turn repeatable advisory work into products or subscription tools, not to keep selling bespoke days.
- **Pricing**: the audit, blueprint, and advisor offers should be priced as outcomes, not days. The price should reflect the value of the consolidated system, not the time spent building it.
- **Key person of influence**: this is exactly the positioning AlphaWGA is building. The website, content, and LinkedIn presence should make Bamidele the person people think of when operations are scattered.
