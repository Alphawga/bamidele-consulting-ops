# Daniel Priestley: The $1 Million Landing Page (Works For Normal People)

Raw source: `../raw/2026-07-19-daniel-priestley-the-1-million-landing-page-works-for-normal-people.md`.

## Core thesis

Everything is downstream from lead generation. If you can generate leads, you can solve every other problem. If you cannot, you are dead in the water. The highest-leverage lead-generation asset is an online assessment or scorecard: a landing page, a 15-question quiz, and a dynamic results page that qualifies the lead and points them to the right next step.

## The landing page

A high-converting landing page has four parts:

1. **Hook** — one of two types:
   - **Frustration hook**: "Feeling frustrated that [pain] even though [effort]."
   - **Results hook**: "Are you ready to [desired outcome]?"
2. **Subheading** — direct the visitor to the assessment: "Answer 15 questions to find out why you're experiencing this frustration and what to do about it."
3. **Value proposition** — "Take this assessment so we can measure and improve three key areas: X, Y, Z." The three areas should be intermediate outcomes on the way to the main result.
4. **Credibility** — who created it, your background, and the research or statistics behind it.
5. **Call to action** — "Start the quiz. It only takes 3 minutes. It's completely free. You get immediate recommendations on how you can improve."

If this is right, 20 to 40% of landing-page visitors will start the quiz.

## The 15 questions

The questionnaire has three parts:

1. **Contact information**: name and email are mandatory; phone is optional; location is captured automatically.
2. **Ten best-practices questions**: these determine the score. They ask whether the prospect is doing the things they should be doing (e.g., tracking calories, working out three times a week, getting to bed on time).
3. **Five qualification questions** that tell you how to sell to them:
   - Which best describes your current situation?
   - Which describes your desired outcome in the next 90 days?
   - What is the obstacle you think is stopping you, or what have you tried that hasn't worked?
   - Which solution do you think would suit you best? (education, coaching, software, done-for-you — this signals budget)
   - Is there anything else you think we need to know about? (open box; people often reveal urgency, budget, or intent)

## The dynamic results page

The results page changes based on answers. It should include:

- **The big reveal**: the overall score or result.
- **Three insights**: what they should know based on their answers.
- **Next steps**: matched to qualification level. Highly qualified leads get a one-to-one meeting; medium leads get a group event or presentation; poor-fit leads get content (video, podcast, book recommendation).
- **Contact information and social links** so they can explore further.

## Traffic and scale

Priestley treats traffic as a separate topic. The assessment is the conversion engine; the traffic video is about how to send paid and organic attention to the landing page.

## How this maps to AlphaWGA

- The AlphaWGA offer ladder already has a free scorecard as the first rung. This video validates the architecture and gives a precise template for improving it.
- **Hook**: the current scorecard headline should be tested against both the frustration and results formats. The core line "I consolidate scattered business operations into one intelligent system" is closer to a results hook; a frustration hook might be "Feeling frustrated that your operations are scattered across tools even though you're already paying for software."
- **Subheading**: add the exact 15-question framing to the scorecard landing page.
- **Value proposition**: the scorecard should explicitly name the three areas it measures and improves (e.g., visibility, workflow, decision-making).
- **Credibility**: add a short bio and research/statistics section to the scorecard page.
- **15 questions**: the current intake can be upgraded from a generic form to a 10-question best-practices score plus 5 qualification questions. The qualification questions map directly onto the diagnostic and audit offers.
- **Dynamic results**: the scorecard currently may produce a generic result. It should branch into at least three paths: book a diagnostic call (high fit), join a group event or download a blueprint (medium fit), read/watch content (low fit).
- **Budget signal**: the "which solution suits you best" question is a low-friction way to segment prospects into free-content, diagnostic, audit, or advisor tracks.
- The Scorecard Marketing/Scoreapp pitch is vendor-specific; the underlying framework is what matters. The same system can be built with any form tool + automation.
