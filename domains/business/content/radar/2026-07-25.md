## ★ 1. US imposes 12.5% tariff on Nigerian imports — took effect Thursday

**Hook:** A 12.5% tariff on everything Nigeria sells to the US landed Thursday morning. Procurement teams tracking costs in last month's spreadsheets are already behind.

**Context:** The Office of the US Trade Representative announced the tariff on July 23, effective 12:01 a.m. Eastern Time on July 24. Nigeria is among 54 economies found to have failed to enforce bans on goods produced with forced labor. The investigation covered 60 of the US's largest trading partners, took six months, drew over 1,600 written submissions, and involved 100 witnesses. India, the UK, and Indonesia received a lower 10% rate after committing to enforcement reforms. Nigeria did not. The tariff applies under Section 301 of the Trade Act and has no announced sunset date.

**His angle:** Every landed cost calculation that touched the US changed overnight. If a procurement team sources materials internationally and competes on price to US buyers, their cost baseline is now wrong. A connected procurement system updates unit costs, vendor pricing, and margin assumptions in one place when the tariff environment shifts. Companies still running costs through spreadsheets find out at month-end when the invoice arrives and it is 12.5% above forecast. The gap between a connected system and a scattered one is now priced into every export quote.

## ★ 2. Nigeria Revenue Service: large taxpayers have six days to get e-invoicing live

**Hook:** July 31 is the hard deadline for every Nigerian company above N5 billion in gross turnover to have e-invoicing running. That is six days from today.

**Context:** The Nigeria Revenue Service set July 31, 2026 as the cutoff for all large taxpayers to complete onboarding, integration, testing, and live invoice transmission on the national Merchant Buyer Solution platform. E-invoices must be in XML or JSON format, digitally signed, Peppol BIS 3.0 compliant, and submitted with an Invoice Reference Number and cryptographic stamp from the NRS. Medium taxpayers (N1-5B turnover) should have been live since July 1. Compliance monitoring is already active. Financial penalties are announced for 2027, but enforcement activity starts now. As of Q1 this year, over 1,000 companies had completed onboarding. The NRS is watching who has not.

**His angle:** Software onboarding takes one week. Cleaning financial data takes months. Companies scrambling to hit July 31 are solving the wrong problem first. If your sales records, purchase history, and invoice archive live in five different places, e-invoicing compliance becomes a forensic exercise before it becomes a technology exercise. The NRS deadline is a mirror. It shows you exactly how organized your back-office actually is. The companies that pass this without pain are the ones whose records were already clean and in one system.

## ★ 3. NUPRC awards 50 oil and gas blocks — 143 companies competed, every block triggers a Nigerian Content Plan

**Hook:** Nigeria's biggest oil block licensing round since the PIA closed on July 21. 143 companies submitted 200 bids. Every block awarded now starts a procurement cycle with mandatory local content requirements.

**Context:** NUPRC held the commercial bid conference on July 21 at the Transcorp Hilton in Abuja. Only companies that cleared technical prequalification were admitted. The 50 blocks span the Niger Delta (onshore and shallow water), Anambra, Chad, and Benue basins. The government projects $10 billion in new upstream investment and up to 400,000 barrels per day of additional production when blocks reach full operational capacity. Under the Petroleum Industry Act and NCDMB rules, block winners must submit Nigerian Content Plans, maintain local vendor registers, and document local spend from day one of their operations.

**His angle:** Every block awarded is a procurement trigger. New operators need vendors. To get onto a vendor list for a new block, an indigenous company needs financial records, HSE certifications, technical capacity documentation, and past performance data, ready to submit on short notice. The NCDMB capacity audit announced at NOG 2026 and these new block awards are connected events. The indigenous suppliers who pass the audit will be the ones qualifying for these new contracts. The documentation problem is the same in both cases: scattered records mean missed opportunities.

## 4. Domino: 57% of enterprises still can't make AI investment pay back — two years in a row

**Hook:** Two years running, more than half of enterprises that invest in AI spend more than they get back. The productivity numbers are real. The ROI numbers are honest. They are different problems.

**Context:** Domino Data Lab's Fifth Annual Enterprise AI Report, published July 22, surveyed 639 senior enterprise AI leaders across North America, the UK, and Europe (BARC Research, April 2026). Key findings: 57% of enterprises report ROI that fails to outpace AI investment, a number unchanged from 2025. Yet 93% report improved production capability, up from 88% last year. The report identifies a "last-mile gap" between AI models running in production and business users who cannot access or act on the outputs. On the agentic side: expanding AI agent use is the top priority for enterprise AI leaders in 2026, yet 41% of organizations are running or scaling agentic AI without governance in place.

**His angle:** The productivity gains are real. The ROI gap exists because those gains are locked inside technical teams, invisible to the business. AI that makes a data scientist faster does not show up in the CFO's numbers. The same logic runs in operations: AI layered on top of fragmented records generates reports nobody trusts. AI connected to one integrated data source generates actions everyone can act on. The companies that will close the ROI gap will do it by fixing the data layer before adding the next agent, not the other way round.

## 5. Nearpays: startup from Abuja beats 500 companies to win the UN's top AI prize

**Hook:** A company from Abuja just beat more than 500 global competitors for the United Nations' top AI innovation prize. The product turns any Android phone into a contactless payment terminal.

**Context:** Nearpays won the AI for Good Innovation Factory grand finale in Geneva on July 20, becoming the first African startup to win the competition outright. The company built an AI-powered SoftPOS platform that transforms Android smartphones into NFC contactless payment terminals, removing the hardware cost barrier for small merchants. Victor Daniyan, founder and CEO, based out of Abuja, took the company from winning the African regional in Johannesburg to the global final. Prize: $20,000 plus access to a global network of investors, policymakers, and research partners supporting AI solutions with real-world impact.

**His angle:** The Nearpays win is a useful data point beyond the pride moment. The solution works because it runs on infrastructure merchants already own, not infrastructure they need to buy. That is the same principle as business consolidation: don't add a new tool to the stack, connect and activate what is already there. Also notable: this AI solves a physical operations problem, the cost of payment acceptance hardware, not a language or search problem. That is the category of AI worth watching in 2026. It removes friction from operations rather than producing more output from existing friction.

## 6. Dangote priced fuel in dollars, NNPCL says it delivered everything it had — both are reading different systems

**Hook:** Nigeria's biggest refinery and its state crude supplier disagree on how much oil was actually delivered. The argument is not about honesty. It is about two systems with different numbers.

**Context:** Under the naira-for-crude programme, NNPCL says it allocated 100% of available naira-denominated crude to Dangote Refinery. Dangote says it received three of the fourteen cargoes it expected. With insufficient naira crude, the refinery began sourcing from international traders at dollar prices and passed those costs into product pricing, sparking a standoff with the government over fuel sold in USD. The dispute moved to litigation on some fronts in Q2 2026. Nigeria's domestic refineries received 28.5 million barrels in the first half, against an allocation of 61.9 million, roughly 46% of what was planned.

**His angle:** When two parties in the same transaction read different delivery figures from different systems, neither can escalate or resolve with confidence. NNPCL measures against crude that was available to allocate. Dangote measures against cargo nominations and expected volume. Both positions are consistent within their own data environment. That is the definition of a procurement data integrity failure. A connected system records what was ordered, confirmed, delivered, and invoiced in one place. Any gap shows up in hours, not after a public dispute. What Dangote and NNPCL are managing at scale is the same problem a mid-sized procurement team manages when orders live in emails and delivery records live in a separate spreadsheet.
