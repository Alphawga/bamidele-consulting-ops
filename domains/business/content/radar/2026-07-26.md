## ★ 1. Nigeria awards 37 oil and gas blocks — 143 companies competed, $10bn investment expected

**Hook:** Nigeria completed its 2025 oil licensing round this week. Thirty-seven blocks awarded.
The 2026 round is already approved to start.

**Context:** The Nigerian Upstream Petroleum Regulatory Commission held its commercial bid
conference on July 21, 2026. Of 286 companies that applied for prequalification, 196 qualified,
and 143 eventually submitted 200 bids for 50 blocks spanning onshore Niger Delta, shallow water,
deepwater, and frontier basins (Anambra, Chad, Benue Trough). Thirty-seven blocks were awarded;
13 attracted no bids and returned to the licensing basket. The government expects $10 billion in
investments from the round. President Tinubu has already approved the 2026 licensing round to
begin next.

**His angle:** Every new block award is a procurement event that has not started yet. New
operators have to stand up project offices, build vendor rosters, qualify suppliers, and manage
MRO for fields that do not exist yet. The operators who build a connected system from day one
will not need a crisis to prompt the fix later. That is a well-timed window for anyone selling
or advising on operations infrastructure to new block holders. The 2026 round starts next.

## ★ 2. Nigeria crude output hits 74-month high — beats OPEC quota at 104%

**Hook:** Nigeria produced 1.735 million barrels per day of crude and condensates in June 2026.
That is the highest output since April 2020. The OPEC quota is 1.5 million bpd.

**Context:** OPEC and NUPRC data released in July show Nigeria's June production at a 74-month
high. Bonny Terminal contributed 318,000 bpd; Forcados contributed 306,000 bpd. This was the
second consecutive month Nigeria met its OPEC quota after roughly 11 months of missed targets.
The recovery is attributed to reduced pipeline vandalism and improved field management. The 1.56
million bpd crude-only figure was the highest since April 2020.

**His angle:** More production means more procurement activity: more MRO purchases, more vendor
payments, more logistics moving faster. The question for indigenous operators and service
companies around active terminals is whether their procurement systems can match the pace.
Operators whose MRO records are connected can increase order velocity without adding headcount.
Operators still managing purchase orders across email threads will hit a different kind of ceiling
at exactly the moment the opportunity is largest.

## 3. Nigeria external reserves cross $52.73 billion — highest level since 2009

**Hook:** Nigeria's gross external reserves hit $52.73 billion in July. That covers 11 months
of imports. The last time reserves were this high was January 2009.

**Context:** The CBN reported reserves rising from $45.56 billion at the start of 2026 to $52.73
billion by mid-July, a $6.96 billion gain of 15.3% in six months. The main drivers are crude oil
tax inflows and third-party inflows. The CBN's own full-year reserve target of $51 billion has
already been exceeded. At the MPC meeting on July 20-21, the committee held the MPR at 26.5%,
with headline inflation at 15.91% in June.

**His angle:** Strong reserves give the CBN room to defend the Naira. They do not give
individual businesses visibility into their own FX exposure. A procurement team with USD
payables and Naira receivables tracked across separate spreadsheets is still flying blind even
when the macro position is the strongest in 17 years. The macro gives confidence. Your own
system gives control. Those are not the same thing, and only one of them is within your reach
to fix.

## ★ 4. Banks shifted N2.22 trillion more credit to Nigerian businesses in June alone

**Hook:** Credit to Nigeria's private sector rose to N83.26 trillion in June 2026. Government
borrowing dropped by N350 billion the same month. The credit is moving.

**Context:** CBN data published July 23 shows private sector credit rising from N81.04 trillion
in May to N83.26 trillion in June, a 2.8% monthly increase and a 9% year-on-year gain from
N76.13 trillion in June 2025. Government credit fell to N40.03 trillion from N40.38 trillion.
This lending expansion happened while the MPR sits at 26.5%. Banks are increasing commercial
lending, not retrenching.

**His angle:** Credit decisions depend on the quality of financial records. A business whose
purchases, receivables, payables, and bank balances all live in one connected system can produce
its actual financial position in hours. One with scattered records takes days, and the picture
is less accurate. The same work that makes operations cleaner makes the lending application
stronger. Connected accounting is not only an operations tool. It is a lending credential, and
the credit is there for the businesses that can prove their numbers.

## 5. Alterion launches Draco: a runtime control platform built specifically to govern AI agents

**Hook:** A former McKinsey partner and a Google engineering VP just launched a product whose
entire job is to govern the AI agents enterprises have already deployed.

**Context:** Alterion launched Draco on July 16, 2026. It sits between AI agents and enterprise
infrastructure as a dedicated runtime control layer, observing every prompt and action in real
time and applying programmable guardrails before agents take high-risk actions. It targets
compliance with SOC 2, ISO 42001, and the EU AI Act. The product was built in response to a
specific gap: enterprises are deploying agents without a unified way to understand what those
agents are doing or enforce policy at runtime. Most enterprise AI agent projects never make it
past the demo stage because compliance reviews catch what the pilot ignored.

**His angle:** Most companies asking about AI agents are asking the wrong question. They ask
"which agent should we use." The right question is "who governs the agent once it is running."
Draco is proof that this gap is real enough to build a dedicated product around it. For any
Nigerian operator considering AI automation in procurement or operations, the governance layer
comes before the agent, not after the incident. The companies who get AI right will be the ones
who built the control plane first, not the ones who deployed the fastest.

## 6. FreightWaves gives its top AI award to autonomous procurement — not analytics, not dashboards

**Hook:** The 2026 FreightWaves AI Excellence Award went to Trimble for automating end-to-end
spot freight procurement. The nominations doubled year-on-year. That is not a trend piece.
That is a baseline shift.

**Context:** FreightWaves held its 2026 AI Excellence in Supply Chain Awards on July 15 in
Chicago. Trimble Autonomous Procurement won for automating the full process of securing spot
road freight capacity. FreightWaves received 60 nominations, more than double last year's
total. The organizers described the volume increase as a reflection of how quickly AI has moved
from pilot projects to production systems across freight and logistics.

**His angle:** A year ago, "AI in procurement" mostly meant better dashboards. This year there
are enough end-to-end production deployments to run a competitive awards program with a
dedicated autonomous procurement category. The baseline has moved. For Nigerian oil and gas
procurement teams watching this space, Trimble's win is a two-to-three-year signal: the
autonomous procurement practices maturing in US freight today will shape what clients expect
from local procurement operations in that window. The gap between global practice and local
practice is closing faster than most operators plan for.

**Suggested format:** Quick note
