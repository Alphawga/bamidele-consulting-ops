## ★ 1. NRS e-invoicing deadline is July 31: eight days left for large Nigerian businesses

**Hook:** The Nigeria Revenue Service gave large taxpayers until July 31 to go live on the
national e-invoicing platform. Eight days.

**Context:** The NRS set July 31 as the hard deadline for companies with N5bn or more in
annual turnover to complete onboarding on the national Electronic Fiscal System (EFS), also
called the Merchant Buyer Solution. Compliance means full system integration through an
accredited Access Point Provider, active invoice transmission, and the ability to receive only
e-invoices carrying valid Invoice Reference Numbers from all suppliers. Enforcement starts
August 1. A second wave covering N1bn to N5bn businesses is already active; small enterprises
follow in July 2027.

**His angle:** This is not a digital upgrade. It is a connected-records mandate. Every large
business now has to build the pipe between its accounting system and the NRS platform and
verify that its suppliers are sending compliant invoices on the other end. Companies running
invoicing across separate spreadsheets, email chains, and manual approval rounds will fail
validation. The same connected-records argument that runs through every Bamidele Consulting
engagement now has a statutory deadline with penalties attached.

## ★ 2. CBN held rates at 26.5% because of the Middle East, not because of Nigerian inflation

**Hook:** The MPC voted unanimously to hold at 26.5% on July 21. The stated reason was not
domestic data. It was the US-Iran conflict.

**Context:** Nigeria's headline inflation fell marginally to 15.91% in June 2026, down from
15.93% in May. The Monetary Policy Committee met on July 21 and held the Monetary Policy Rate
at 26.5% for the second consecutive meeting. The committee cited renewed Middle East
hostilities and uncertainty around the US economy as the primary reason for caution. The CBN
had signalled rate cuts earlier in the year and reversed course as geopolitical risk
accelerated. The Cash Reserve Ratio for deposit money banks remains at 45%.

**His angle:** Nigeria's cost of borrowing is now a function of a conflict thousands of
kilometres away. For businesses planning procurement cycles, equipment financing, or any
capital spend that relies on credit, the planning horizon just got harder to read. Businesses
that run on tight cash cycles and short vendor-credit terms are exposed in ways that
businesses with longer-term contracted supply arrangements are not. Another data point for the
argument: the businesses that keep their financial and operational picture in one place can
see and respond to this kind of shock faster than the ones still pulling numbers from three
different places at the end of the month.

## ★ 3. SAP is building 200 AI agents and publicly warning about "agentic chaos" in the same breath

**Hook:** SAP plans 200 AI agents across enterprise functions by year-end. Its own survey
found only 14% of organisations have the governance structures to run them safely.

**Context:** At Sapphire 2026, SAP unveiled its Autonomous Enterprise strategy with more than
50 Joule domain agents spanning finance, procurement, HR, and supply chain, and a roadmap to
200 by December. The company simultaneously issued a warning that deploying agents across
enterprise applications without oversight creates "agentic chaos," where autonomous systems
access sensitive data and take actions with no audit trail. SAP's internal data shows 23% of
organisations believe their data is AI-ready, and just 14% believe they have governance
structures ready to scale agents responsibly. SAP's answer is a "verified agent" model
requiring certification before any agent touches its systems.

**His angle:** A major software company ships 200 agents and warns you in the same press
cycle that most organisations cannot safely deploy them. That is not a contradiction. It is a
description of the market. Companies layering agents onto fragmented ERP data, disconnected
procurement records, and informal approval processes are building the chaos SAP is naming.
Governance only works on top of data that has integrity. The companies that built one
connected system first will get ROI from the agents. The rest will be in the 40% Gartner
flagged last week.

## 4. Airtel Money is heading for a $10bn London IPO serving 54 million customers

**Hook:** Airtel Money, with 54 million customers and a 50.8% EBITDA margin, is moving
toward a London listing at a reported $10bn valuation.

**Context:** Airtel Africa is advancing plans to list its mobile money subsidiary in London,
with Citigroup leading the syndicate and additional banks being brought in. The business
generated $1.35bn in revenue in 2026, with an EBITDA margin higher than Airtel Africa's
consolidated operations. The company previously shelved the IPO during market volatility and
considered a Middle East listing before settling on London. Reports from July 20 and 21
confirm the process is active and banks have been formally engaged.

**His angle:** A payment infrastructure business built on interbank rails across Africa is
being valued at enterprise scale. For Nigerian business operators, the practical implication
is not the IPO itself. It is that the payment infrastructure across the continent is
consolidating into fewer, larger, regulated rails. Businesses still reconciling collections
manually are moving in the opposite direction of where the infrastructure is heading. This is
a five-year signal, not a five-day trade.

oil and gas operations.

**Context:** The Oil and Gas Field Readiness Training Programme places 240 trainees in Lagos

engineers enter the market over the next 12 months, the indigenous contractors and service
companies that can track qualifications, onboarding records, and compliance certifications in
one system will absorb the talent without friction. The companies managing HR through a
phone, a notebook, and a spreadsheet will either miss the window or create the documentation
gap that fails the Q3 NCDMB capacity audit.

## 6. NNPC cut $3.4bn in operating costs in 14 months through contract restructuring

**Hook:** NNPC saved $3.4bn in 14 months. The method was contract review and optimisation,
not headcount cuts.

**Context:** NNPC CEO Bayo Ojulari disclosed at the company's one-year performance review
that the company reduced operating costs by $3.4bn through contract restructuring from
April 2025 to June 2026. Crude production rose 6% to 569.7 million barrels during the same
period. NNPC also achieved 100% compliance with joint venture cash call obligations throughout.
The savings came from reviewing existing agreements and eliminating waste across operations.
(Story disclosed July 8, 2026 at NOG Energy Week.)

**His angle:** The largest operator in Nigeria's oil and gas sector saved $3.4bn by looking
at its contracts, not by cutting people. That pressure flows downstream. Indigenous
contractors and MRO suppliers in that same supply chain are operating inside those
renegotiated terms now. The companies that survived the renegotiation are the ones with
transparent cost structures and documented delivery records. You cannot defend a contract
position you cannot measure.
