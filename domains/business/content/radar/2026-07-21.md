## ★ 1. Federal Government raises N729bn bond to settle GenCos' verified legacy debts — Investors' Forum today

**Hook:** The Federal Government is meeting bond investors today to raise N729bn for power
sector debts. Only verified legacy debts are eligible.

**Context:** The Investors' Forum for the second N729bn bond issuance under the Presidential
Power Sector Debt Reduction Programme is scheduled for July 21, 2026. This completes the
first phase of the N4 trillion programme to settle debts owed to electricity generation
companies. The first series (N501bn, issued January 2026) had its inaugural coupon and
principal repayment settled in full on July 14. The programme processes only verified legacy
debts: unverified claims are excluded from each tranche.

**His angle:** "Verified" is doing a lot of work in that word. The generation companies that
had organized receivables records, documented billing history, and clean debt ledgers got
paid in January 2026. The ones still working to verify are waiting for a later tranche. This
is a documentation outcome, not a finance outcome. It happens in procurement too: the vendors
with organized contract histories get paid faster, get selected faster, and pass audits
faster. The discipline of keeping accurate records is what turns a financial event into cash.

## ★ 2. NRS sets July 31 deadline for large taxpayers to adopt e-invoicing — 10 days left

**Hook:** Large taxpayers have 10 days to integrate the NRS Merchant Buyer Solution. The
July 31 deadline is not an extension notice.

**Context:** The Nigeria Revenue Service announced July 19, 2026 that it is now in active
compliance monitoring. All companies with annual gross turnover of N5 billion and above must
fully adopt the national e-invoicing and Electronic Fiscal System, known as the Merchant
Buyer Solution (MBS), by July 31. The system requires integration through approved Access
Point Providers, real-time transmission of invoices to the NRS platform, and completion of
validation and testing. More than 1,000 large taxpayer companies already comply. NRS can
pursue enforcement actions for non-compliance under existing tax laws.

**His angle:** The companies scrambling now have a data problem disguised as a technology
problem. The integration itself is not the hard part. Knowing where every invoice your
business has generated actually lives is. A company generating invoices from three accounting
tools, two project management systems, and a spreadsheet cannot integrate cleanly with any
government platform in 10 days. The ones who passed quickly in Q1 were the ones who already
had invoicing centralized. This is what one connected system looks like when a regulator
comes knocking.

make it onto major JV supplier lists are the ones with auditable, verifiable records. NCDMB's
physical audit and Nigeria First Procurement Policy are both raising the documentation floor.
This workshop shows that floor is now reaching SME and community vendor level.

## ★ 4. CBN caps fintech market dominance: 25% consumer issuing and 15% merchant acquiring cannot sit in one company

**Hook:** The CBN is rewriting the rules on who can control what in Nigeria's payment market.
No firm can simultaneously hold more than 25% of consumer issuing and 15% of merchant
acquiring.

**Context:** A CBN circular from June 15, 2026 introduced market structure requirements,
data localisation rules, and Ultimate Beneficial Ownership disclosure obligations for the
Nigerian payments system. The July 20 TechCabal analysis called it the end of unchecked
expansion for Nigerian fintechs. Firms must submit monthly market-share reports and comply
with the new thresholds by end of 2026. The circular also requires all payment data to be
stored locally.

**His angle:** The payment infrastructure that most businesses rely on is being restructured.
Companies that built their financial operations entirely around one dominant processor will
feel this when that processor restructures to meet the 25%/15% cap. The ones who have their
own clean ledger, with transaction records independent of any single payment provider, will
switch rails without disruption. Financial data should live in your system, not in a payment
provider's database that you access through their interface. This regulation makes that
principle visible.

## 5. Hackett Group 2026: 56% of procurement organizations have deployed agentic AI, ROI is 3.7x

**Hook:** 56% of procurement teams are running agentic AI now. The Hackett Group says those
that are getting 3.7 times the ROI of those that are not.

**Context:** The Hackett Group's 2026 Procurement Key Issues Study found that 56% of
procurement organizations have deployed agentic AI, nearly double last year's rate. 71%
have adopted Gen AI at pilot or large-scale implementation. 76% of adopters report
performance improvements of 25% or more. The study also projects that procurement workloads
will increase 8% in 2026 even as headcount and budgets decline, making the pressure to
automate structural rather than optional.

**His angle:** The 3.7x ROI is real, but read the footnote: it belongs to organizations
that went into AI with connected data. The gains in cycle time reduction and spend
visibility that the study cites are only possible when purchase orders, supplier records,
contracts, and financial data live in one system. AI agents running over four disconnected
spreadsheets will produce faster wrong answers. The 44% of procurement teams not yet using
agentic AI are not late. They are behind on data integration, and that is the actual
bottleneck.
