## ★ 1. Africa's biggest procurement conference is in Lagos today

Victoria Island. The agenda is about resilience, not cost reduction.

**Context:** The 8th Africa Procurement and Supply Chain Conference and Awards (APSCHA 2026)

## ★ 2. 88% of AI agent pilots never reach production — Cognizant launched a team today to fix that

**Hook:** For every 33 AI agent pilots a company starts, four reach production. Cognizant
launched a dedicated EMEA unit today to close that gap, and their diagnosis names the right
problem.

**Context:** Cognizant announced its EMEA AI Unit on July 28, 2026, specifically to help
enterprises across Europe, the Middle East, and Africa move AI agents from pilot to production.
IDC research cited in the announcement found 88% of AI agent proofs-of-concept never reach
broad deployment. Separately, Deloitte found only 1 in 5 companies has a mature governance
model for autonomous agents already in production. The unit offers three service tiers
(Foundation, Accelerate, Transform) spanning AI strategy, governance, and live deployment. It
also includes a "Frontier Deployed Engineering" model built around embedding engineers inside
client operations.

**His angle:** The gap is not in the AI model. It is in the data underneath it. An AI agent
running on fragmented operations data — purchase orders in one spreadsheet, inventory in
another, vendor records scattered across email — will produce fragmented outputs. The 88%
failure rate concentrates in companies that layered AI on top of unconnected systems. The
integration work comes first. Build one connected data layer, then point the AI at it. The
Cognizant launch is a consulting response to a structural problem. The structural fix is still
the client's job to do.

## ★ 3. Nigeria's fintech players have 155 days to restructure — the forum to talk about it is Thursday

**Hook:** OPay, Moniepoint, PalmPay, Paystack, and Flutterwave all face a December 31, 2026
structural deadline. 1,700 people are meeting Thursday to work out what comes next.

**Context:** The 5th Nigeria Fintech Forum runs July 30 at Civic Centre, VI, Lagos, themed
"Finance, Regulation, and the New Operating Reality." The backdrop: between March and June 2026,
CBN issued what analysts describe as the most sweeping set of fintech regulations in Nigeria's
history across five circulars. The December 31 deadline imposes three changes on major payment
companies. First, market share caps: any institution controlling more than 25% of the
consumer-issuing market is restricted to 15% in merchant-acquiring activities. Second,
operational ring-fencing: each fintech subsidiary must operate with its own governance, capital,
and risk systems (shared infrastructure is no longer acceptable). Third, a data localization
directive requires all customer and transaction data to be held on Nigerian infrastructure.

**His angle:** The CBN is mandating from the outside what I help businesses build from the
inside: clean separation of systems, visible operations, traceable data. Companies that already
have connected, legible internal structures will absorb these changes faster than those still
pooling accounts and spreadsheets across subsidiaries. The operational maturity question is
the same whether the pressure comes from a regulator or a board. The deadline is 155 days away.

## 4. The oil and gas park Nigeria has been building in Bayelsa opens in about 90 days

**Hook:** Nigeria's first purpose-built manufacturing park for oil and gas components goes
operational in Q4 2026. The capacity audit and the park opening are on the same timeline.

**Context:** The Nigerian Oil and Gas Park Scheme (NOGaPS) at Emeyal-1, Ogbia Local Government
Area, Bayelsa State is on track for Q4 2026 launch. NCDMB confirmed completion of manufacturing
shop floors, water treatment infrastructure, accommodation, and power infrastructure including a
2.5-megawatt CNG power plant. The park is designed to allow Nigerian companies to manufacture
components and equipment that the oil and gas sector currently imports. Over 2,000 jobs are
expected at full operationalization.

**His angle:** This park changes the vendor landscape for procurement professionals. A supplier
that can show manufactured-in-Nigeria products from a purpose-built facility carries a different
weight in a bid than a trading company sourcing the same product abroad. The timing intersects
directly with the Q3 capacity audit already underway: companies audited now without demonstrable
manufacturing capacity will watch contracts flow to NOGaPS operators by Q1 2027. Procurement
teams should map which of their current and potential vendors are positioning to operate there.
That vendor intelligence needs to live alongside contract records and certification files in one
connected system, not in a separate spreadsheet.

## 5. Nigeria's startup ecosystem: 7,535 shutdowns later, the survivors are building infrastructure

**Hook:** Nearly 7,500 Nigerian startups have closed. The ones still operating have stopped
chasing consumers and started building the infrastructure underneath commerce.

**Context:** 2026 data from Nigeria's startup ecosystem counts 24,967 companies, 1,630 funded,
$30.7 billion raised across the ecosystem's life, 5 unicorns, and 7,535 shutdowns. The
surviving cohort's focus has shifted explicitly from consumer applications to infrastructure:
payments rails, merchant tools, mobility systems, energy access, healthcare supply chains, and
practical business software for operators. This is described in the July 2026 roundup as the
"infrastructure-first" phase of Nigerian startup development, replacing what the report calls
"consumer app hype."

**His angle:** This is the same consolidation dynamic that plays out inside enterprises. The
early-phase rush to build new things gives way to the harder question: what connects the things
that already exist? The infrastructure-first companies are building connective tissue for
Nigerian commerce. Businesses they serve will benefit directly. For any operator evaluating
their tech stack right now, the tools becoming available in Nigeria are increasingly designed
for operations, not consumers. The market is catching up to the problem I have been working on
from the consulting side.
