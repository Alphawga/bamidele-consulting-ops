# The AlphaWGA Offer Playbook

Offer ladder, delivery guides, and brand architecture.

*One ladder. One niche. Advice first, software later.*

## 1. Brand Architecture: The Person and The Business

*Bamidele Ajibola is the person people trust. AlphaWGA is the business that delivers. Keep
the jobs separate and the whole ladder gets easier to sell and easier to scale.*

**The rule:** Bamidele Ajibola sells. AlphaWGA delivers. People buy from a person. They pay,
sign, and stay with a company.

| | Bamidele Ajibola (the person) | AlphaWGA (the business) |
|---|---|---|
| Job | Authority, trust, storytelling | Delivery, contracts, money |
| Lives on | LinkedIn profile, YouTube channel, speaking, DMs | Website, proposals, invoices, reports, email domain |
| Owns | The audience, the voice, the relationships | The IP, case studies, templates, future team, SaaS products |
| Content | Stories, lessons, opinions, Okoh receipts | Case studies, service pages, client documents |
| Signs off as | "Bamidele Ajibola, Founder, AlphaWGA" | "The AlphaWGA team" on documents |
| Survives | Any pivot. The person outlives every offer | This chapter of the mission |

### Why this split matters

- Trust converts on LinkedIn and YouTube through a face, not a logo. Voice rules (one person
  talking to one person) only work as Bamidele.
- A ₦1.5M invoice from AlphaWGA on letterhead feels like hiring a firm. The same invoice from
  a personal account feels like paying a freelancer. Positioning is priced in.
- When SkinAI or SaaS #2 grows, it sits beside or under AlphaWGA. The personal brand never has
  to be rebuilt when the business evolves.
- Referrals travel better with a company name. Okoh can say "talk to AlphaWGA" in a boardroom
  without it sounding like a favour to a friend.

### Setup checklist

- Confirm CAC registration for AlphaWGA (business name or LTD). If not done, this is week one.
  A corporate bank account follows it.
- Set up alphawga.com email (hello@ and bamidele@) on Google Workspace. Client documents never
  come from Gmail.
- Website is AlphaWGA branded, with face and story on it. The About page is where the two
  brands shake hands.
- Create the AlphaWGA LinkedIn company page. Keep it thin: services, case studies, reposts of
  personal content. The personal profile stays the engine.
- All proposals, invoices, reports, and NDAs carry AlphaWGA letterhead. Build the templates
  once, in the brand palette.
- Every piece of personal content signs off the same way: Bamidele Ajibola, Founder, AlphaWGA.
  Repetition welds the two names together.

## 2. The Offer Ladder

*Five rungs. Each one earns the right to sell the next. Nobody is pitched two rungs above
where they stand.*

| Rung | Offer | Price | Job in the ladder |
|---|---|---|---|
| 0. Free | Operations Self-Audit Scorecard | Free (DM keyword) | Turn followers into leads. Create the "I have a problem" moment |
| 1. Filter | Diagnostic Call | ₦10,000 | Filter serious buyers. Diagnose one leak live |
| 2. Anchor | Systems Audit | ₦250,000 | Full leak map and roadmap. The core website offer |
| 3. Premium | Consolidation Blueprint | ₦750,000–₦1.5M | Full future-state design their team can implement |
| 4. Recurring | Fractional Systems Advisor | ₦300,000–₦500,000/mo | Retained advisory. Monthly recurring revenue |
| Parked | Custom ERP build | From ₦3M (2027 waitlist) | Not for sale now. Audits and blueprints fill the 2027 pipeline |

### The money math

Per month at modest volume: 4 diagnostic calls (₦40K), 2 audits (₦500K), 1 blueprint every 6
weeks (~₦650K/month averaged), 2 retainers running (₦700K). That's roughly ₦1.9M/month from
advisory alone, with zero production code written. It funds the Okoh and SkinAI builds instead
of competing with them.

### Conversion assumptions to test

- Scorecard downloads → diagnostic bookings: aim for 10–15%.
- Diagnostic → audit: aim for 40–50%. The call is designed to convert.
- Audit → blueprint or retainer: aim for 30–40%. The report is designed to convert.
- Track these three numbers weekly in the income review. They tell you which rung is broken.

## 3. Rung 0: Operations Self-Audit Scorecard (Free)

**What it is:** A 20-question self-scoring checklist. A business owner rates their own
operation across 5 areas and gets a score band that names their level of chaos.

**Who it is for:** Any Nigerian SME owner who sees the content. Procurement and oil and gas
language in examples, but broad enough for every follower.

**Promise:** "Find out where your business is leaking money in 10 minutes."

**Format:** A well-designed PDF (AlphaWGA branded) plus an optional Google Sheet that
auto-scores.

### The 5 sections (4 questions each, scored 1–5)

1. **Quotations and sales** — how quotes get made, tracked, followed up, converted.
2. **Inventory and delivery** — where stock levels live, how orders are tracked to delivery.
3. **Money** — invoicing speed, receivables tracking, how the owner knows profit per job.
4. **People and tasks** — how work is assigned, chased, and confirmed done.
5. **Data and memory** — what happens when a key staff member is absent. Where the business
   brain lives.

### Score bands

- 80–100: Consolidated. You are the exception. (Almost nobody scores here, and they know it.)
- 55–79: Held together by heroes. The business runs on 2–3 people's memory.
- 30–54: Leaking daily. Money is being lost in handoffs and follow-ups.
- Below 30: Flying blind. The owner is the operating system, and the owner is tired.

### How to build it (one weekend)

- Draft the 20 questions from the Okoh V1 discovery notes. Every question should describe a
  pain personally fixed before.
- Design the PDF in the brand palette. Last page: score bands, one Okoh proof line, and the
  CTA to book a Diagnostic Call.
- Set up delivery: DM keyword (comment or DM "SCORECARD") via manual reply at first. Automate
  later. Also a download form on the website (name + email + WhatsApp).
- Store every lead in one simple sheet: name, email, score if shared, date, source post.

### How to deliver it professionally

- Reply to every SCORECARD DM within a few hours with the file and one line: "Fill it
  honestly. Tell me your score if you want my read on it."
- When someone shares a score, give a genuinely useful 2–3 line read. No pitch yet. This
  exchange is the product.
- Follow up once, 3 days later, with a relevant question about their worst section. Curiosity,
  not pressure.

### Moving them to Rung 1

> Ascension line: "Your score puts you in the leaking-daily band. If you want, I do a
> 45-minute diagnostic where we find the single most expensive leak and what it costs you
> monthly. It is ₦10K, and it counts toward any work we do after. Want the link?"

*Red flag: anyone who wants free consulting in the DMs beyond the score read. Answer once,
warmly, then point to the diagnostic. The ₦10K is the filter working.*

## 4. Rung 1: Diagnostic Call (₦10,000)

**What it is:** A paid 45-minute video call that finds one expensive operational leak and
puts a monthly cost figure on it.

**Who it is for:** Scorecard leads, content leads, and referrals not yet warm enough for the
audit.

**Promise:** "In 45 minutes you will know your most expensive operational leak and what it
costs you every month."

**Price logic:** ₦10K is not revenue. It is a seriousness filter and a commitment device.
Fully credited toward the Systems Audit if booked within 7 days.

### Before the call

- Booking runs through Cal.com with Paystack payment attached. No payment, no booking. Zero
  manual chasing.
- Intake form at booking: what the business does, team size, the 3 tools or methods they run
  on (WhatsApp counts), the one thing that frustrates them most, monthly revenue band
  (optional).
- Spend 10 minutes before the call reviewing the intake and writing a hypothesis: which leak
  is likely biggest.

### Call structure (45 minutes)

- **0–5 min:** Frame it. "Here is how this works: you talk, I dig, and by the end you will
  have one number and one next step." Professional, warm, in charge.
- **5–15 min:** Their story. Let them describe a typical order from enquiry to cash. Take
  notes in their own words. Do not interrupt with solutions.
- **15–35 min:** Dig into ONE leak. Pick the biggest one and quantify it together: hours lost
  weekly, deals that went cold, stock bought twice, invoices sent late. Multiply into a
  monthly naira figure with them watching the math happen. This number is the whole product.
- **35–42 min:** Give 2 quick wins they can do without you, this week. Be generous. This is
  what they screenshot and tell their friends about.
- **42–45 min:** The next step. See ascension script below. One clear option, not a menu.

### After the call (within 24 hours)

- Send a one-page Diagnostic Summary on AlphaWGA letterhead: their leak, the monthly cost
  figure, the 2 quick wins, and the audit offer with the ₦10K credit and 7-day window. This
  document does the selling after you leave.
- Log the call in the pipeline sheet: leak found, cost figure, objections heard, next step
  agreed.

### Moving them to Rung 2

> Ascension line: "Today we found one leak worth about ₦X monthly. In a full Systems Audit I
> spend two weeks inside your operation, interview your key staff, and map every leak with a
> cost attached, plus the exact roadmap to fix them in order. It is ₦250K, your ₦10K from
> today comes off it, and you get the audit report whether or not we ever work together
> again. Should I send the details?"

*Red flags: wants you to fix things on the diagnostic call itself; disputes the ₦10K; books
but skips the intake form. Each one predicts a painful client. The ladder exists to catch
this early.*

## 5. Rung 2: Systems Audit (₦250,000)

**What it is:** A 2-week diagnostic of the whole operation, delivered as a written Leak Map
and Roadmap plus a 60-minute readout call. This is the anchor offer on the website.

**Who it is for:** Businesses doing roughly ₦5M+ monthly where leaks are visibly costing real
money. Procurement, supply chain, and oil and gas services are home turf.

**Promise:** "In two weeks you will know exactly where your operation leaks money, what each
leak costs monthly, and the order to fix them in."

**Payment:** 100% upfront, invoiced by AlphaWGA. No exceptions. An audit with an outstanding
balance is a hostage negotiation.

### Week 1: Discovery

- **Day 1:** Kickoff call (60 min) with the owner. Confirm scope, book staff interviews,
  request samples: 5 recent quotes, a stock record, 2 months of invoices, any tracking sheets.
  Send a checklist so they know exactly what to gather.
- **Days 2–4:** Interview 2–3 key staff, 45 minutes each (the person who quotes, the person
  who tracks orders, the person who chases money). Ask each one: "Walk me through yesterday."
  The gap between the owner's version and the staff version is where the leaks live.
- **Day 5:** Tool and data walkthrough. Screen-share session where they show how a real order
  actually moves. Collect screenshots for the report (with permission).

### Week 2: Analysis and delivery

- **Days 6–8:** Build the Leak Map. Every leak gets: description in their own words, root
  cause, monthly cost estimate, and fix difficulty (quick win / project / structural).
- **Days 9–10:** Write the report, 15–20 pages, AlphaWGA branded. Structure: how the
  operation runs today (their process, drawn as a diagram), the Leak Map, the Cost of Chaos
  total (one big monthly number), the Roadmap (fixes in sequence, each marked DIY / any
  developer / AlphaWGA), and the appendix with interview evidence.
- **Readout call (60 min):** walk the owner through the report. Spend most of the time on the
  Cost of Chaos number and the first 3 roadmap items. End with the ascension script.

### Delivery standards that make it feel premium

- Everything scheduled at kickoff. The client should never wonder what happens next.
- A 2-line WhatsApp update at the end of each week-1 day: "Done with interviews today. Two
  big findings already. Report on track."
- The report is designed, not just written. Brand palette, real diagrams, their numbers. This
  document will be shown to their partners and friends. It is your best salesman.
- Never pad. If the operation is healthier than expected, say so and shrink the roadmap.
  Honesty here buys the retainer later.

### Moving them to Rung 3 or 4

> Ascension line (end of readout): "The roadmap is yours either way. You have three ways to
> execute it: your team does it alone using this document; I design the full system for your
> team or any developer to build, which is the Consolidation Blueprint; or I stay on as your
> systems advisor monthly and we work through it together. Based on what I have seen, I would
> recommend [one option]. Want me to send that proposal?"

*Rule: recommend ONE path, not the menu. Owners with in-house or hireable tech capacity get
the Blueprint. Owners who need a thinking partner more than a document get the Retainer.
Never recommend the custom build; if they ask, it goes on the 2027 waitlist.*

## 6. Rung 3: Consolidation Blueprint (₦750,000–₦1.5M)

**What it is:** A 4–6 week engagement producing a complete future-state system design that
the client's team, or any competent developer, can implement without you writing code.

**Who it is for:** Audit clients whose roadmap justifies real system change and who have (or
can hire) implementation capacity.

**Promise:** "A complete design of your consolidated operation: every process mapped, every
tool chosen, every step specified, so any good developer can build it right the first time."

**Pricing:** ₦750K for a single-department scope (e.g. quotation-to-delivery). ₦1.5M for
full-operation scope. Payment: 50% to start, 50% at handover.

### Delivery plan

- **Week 1:** Deep process mapping. Extend the audit findings into full current-state maps.
  Workshop with the owner: what should the business look like in 12 months? Design
  constraints agreed (budget, team, timeline).
- **Weeks 2–3:** Future-state design. The consolidated process drawn end to end. Tool
  selection with honest trade-offs (off-the-shelf vs configured vs custom, with naira costs).
  Data model: what information lives where, who owns it. Role changes: who does what
  differently.
- **Week 4:** The implementation spec. Written so a developer never met could build from it:
  modules, screens, fields, rules, integrations, phases. This is where 8 years of engineering
  becomes the moat; no pure consultant can write this document.
- **Weeks 5–6 (full scope):** Vendor shortlist with vetting questions, phased rollout plan
  with a budget, and a risk list (what breaks first and how to notice).
- **Handover session (90 min):** walk owner plus their implementer through everything. Then
  30 days of async Q&A support included, on WhatsApp or email, so the blueprint does not die
  in a drawer.

### Delivery standards

- Weekly 30-minute checkpoint call, same day and time each week. Send a 3-line summary after
  each.
- Deliverables arrive as one bound document plus editable diagrams. AlphaWGA branded
  throughout.
- Every recommendation carries a cost and a reason. "Use X because Y, costs about Z monthly."
  Owners trust numbers, not adjectives.
- If during the work the best option turns out to be software that would be built in 2027,
  say exactly that and put them on the waitlist formally. Honesty converts better than any
  pitch.

### Moving them onward

> Ascension line (at handover): "Implementation is where blueprints usually die, not because
> the plan is wrong but because nobody senior is watching. If you want, I stay on as your
> systems advisor while your team builds: two calls a month, I review everything before it
> goes live, and your developer gets direct access to me. ₦300K monthly, minimum three months.
> That is the difference between owning this document and living it."

*Also: at handover, ask for the referral. "Who else do you know running an operation like
this? The audit is the easiest way for me to help them." Okoh partnership rules apply: enter
referral conversations together where possible.*

## 7. Rung 4: Fractional Systems Advisor (₦300,000–₦500,000/month)

**What it is:** Retained monthly advisory. The part-time head of systems: the senior
technical brain they cannot afford to hire full-time.

**Who it is for:** Blueprint clients in implementation, audit clients who need a thinking
partner, and (formalised in writing) Okoh post-V2.

**Promise:** "A senior systems head on your leadership team for less than a junior
developer's salary."

**Terms:** Minimum 3-month commitment, paid monthly in advance. ₦300K standard, ₦500K where
also managing vendors or reviewing a build. Auto-renews unless cancelled with 30 days notice.

### What each month includes (cap the scope, in writing)

- Two 60-minute strategy calls per month, scheduled at month start.
- Async access on WhatsApp or email with a 24-hour response promise on business days.
- Review of anything systems-related before they commit: vendor quotes, developer output,
  tool choices, process changes.
- A monthly one-page Systems Report: what improved, what is stuck, what to do next month.
  This report is renewal insurance; it makes the value visible.
- Hard cap of roughly 8–10 hours monthly. Beyond that is a separate proposal. Scope creep is
  how retainers die.

### How to deliver it professionally

- Month start: 15 minutes of prep per client. Review last month's report, set the two call
  agendas.
- Run calls with an agenda sent the day before. Lead the calls; they are renting your
  structure as much as your knowledge.
- Log every async answer that took real thought. These become both the monthly report and
  future content.
- Month end: send the Systems Report plus next month's invoice together. Value statement and
  bill in the same envelope, always.
- Quarter end: a 30-minute review against the roadmap from their audit or blueprint. Show
  distance travelled. Then re-anchor: what are the next 90 days about?

### Renewal and growth

> Renewal line (2 weeks before quarter end): "Quarter ends on the 30th. Here is what changed
> in 90 days: [three concrete wins from the reports]. Next quarter I want us to focus on
> [next roadmap item]. Same terms. Shall I send the renewal invoice?"

*Capacity rule: maximum 4 retainer clients while Okoh V2 and SkinAI are live. At 4, raise the
price for the next one. Scarcity is real here, so price it.*

## 8. The Ascension System: Moving Clients Up

*The ladder only makes money if people climb it. Ascension is designed, not hoped for.*

### The four principles

1. **Every deliverable sells the next rung inside it.** The scorecard's last page sells the
   diagnostic. The diagnostic summary sells the audit. The audit's roadmap sells the
   blueprint and retainer. The monthly report sells the renewal. Never pitch cold; the
   document already did it.
2. **Credits pull people upward.** ₦10K diagnostic credits into the audit (7-day window). For
   audit clients who sign a blueprint within 30 days, credit ₦100K of the audit fee.
   Deadlines are real or they are nothing.
3. **Recommend one path.** After every engagement, name the single next step you would take
   in their shoes. Menus create stalls; recommendations create decisions.
4. **A no is a lead, not a loss.** Every decline goes into a follow-up list. One useful,
   no-pressure check-in at 30 days: share a relevant win or resource, then ask one question
   about their worst leak. Procurement buyers move slowly and remember who stayed useful.

### The pipeline sheet (track weekly)

| Stage | What to record | Weekly question |
|---|---|---|
| Leads | Scorecard downloads, DMs, referrals | How many new, from which post? |
| Diagnostics | Booked, held, leak + cost found | What is the booking rate from leads? |
| Audits | Proposals out, signed, in delivery | What is diagnostic-to-audit conversion? |
| Premium | Blueprints and retainers active | What is audit-to-premium conversion? |
| Follow-ups | Every no with a next-touch date | Who is due a useful check-in? |

This review is already in the weekly cadence (income numbers review). Same sitting, 20
minutes.

## 9. Operations: Running It Like a Firm

### The toolkit (all in the existing stack)

- Cal.com plus Paystack: paid diagnostic bookings, no manual payment chasing.
- Google Workspace on alphawga.com: email, client folders (one per client: intake,
  interviews, deliverables, invoices).
- Resend or simple email: scorecard delivery and the follow-up touches.
- One pipeline spreadsheet until it hurts. No CRM yet. Tools are not the bottleneck; volume
  is.
- Templates to build once, this month: proposal, invoice, diagnostic summary, audit report
  skeleton, blueprint skeleton, monthly systems report. Six documents, all AlphaWGA
  letterhead, all reusable forever.

### Payment rules

- Diagnostic: paid at booking. Audit: 100% upfront. Blueprint: 50/50. Retainer: monthly in
  advance.
- All money enters the AlphaWGA corporate account. The person never invoices.
- Prices are firm for 2026. If demand outruns capacity, raise prices rather than adding
  delivery hours. That rule protects Okoh V2 and SkinAI.

### Website structure (alphawga.com)

- Home: the consolidation thesis, Okoh proof, one CTA (the Systems Audit).
- One page per paid offer: Audit (anchor, most detail), Blueprint, Advisor. Diagnostic call
  is the CTA on all three, not its own page.
- Free scorecard: a simple download page that every content CTA points to.
- About: Bamidele's story, the person behind the firm. This page is where the two brands
  meet.
- Custom software: one honest line. "AlphaWGA takes a limited number of build engagements.
  The 2027 waitlist opens through the Systems Audit." Scarcity, stated plainly, true.

### 30-day launch sequence

- **Week 1:** CAC and bank sorted (if pending). Build the scorecard. Set up Cal.com plus
  Paystack. Draft the 6 templates.
- **Week 2:** Audit and blueprint pages written for the website. Announce the scorecard in
  content. Start replying to SCORECARD DMs.
- **Week 3:** First diagnostic calls. Refine the intake form and call structure after each
  one. Offer the audit using the script.
- **Week 4:** First audit sold or in delivery. Review the three conversion numbers. Adjust
  one thing, not five.

### The one warning

> Do not redesign this ladder for 90 days. The pattern is to go wider when the leverage is in
> going deeper. The ladder is decided. The only work now is volume: content daily, scorecard
> out, calls booked, audits delivered. Review the numbers in October and change what the
> numbers say to change.
