### 4. One refinery operator had $285M across seven storerooms with no cross-site visibility. $46M was recoverable in eleven weeks.

**Date:** 2026 case study

**Hook:** The operator was not buying too much. It was buying the same things twice, at emergency freight rates, without knowing it.

**Context:** A case study from Verdantis documents a major independent oil and gas operator running seven isolated SAP instances across refineries, each site procuring independently with no cross-site stock visibility. Emergency procurement events carried 50 to 80% freight premiums on parts already sitting idle at peer sites. Deploying a single AI-native MRO intelligence layer across all seven sites released $46M in working capital and cut emergency procurement by 44% in eleven weeks. Africa-specific MRO literature notes import lead times of weeks or months make this kind of fragmentation especially costly in the region.

**His angle:** This is the scattered-to-one problem, costed out. When procurement systems do not share a view, companies pay emergency freight on things already in their own storeroom three sites away. The fix is not another inventory tool. It is one connected view across all sites, checked before the purchase order is created.

**Format:** Article or LinkedIn post (strong concrete numbers, worth the longer format)

**Source:** https://www.verdantis.com/oil-gas-refinery-mro-optimization/

### 5. Nigeria ranks 6th globally for worker AI literacy, 19th for enterprise adoption. The gap is the largest on record.

**Date:** June 19, 2026

**Hook:** Nigerian workers are teaching themselves AI faster than their employers are adopting it. That gap has a direct cost.

**Context:** The 2026 Ataraxis Global Outsourcing AI Readiness Index scores Nigeria's workforce at 66 for AI literacy, placing it 6th globally. Enterprise AI adoption scores 34, placing it 19th. The 32-point gap between workforce capability and enterprise deployment is the largest disparity recorded across all 25 countries in the index. Infrastructure explains part of it: 52% broadband penetration, 4% 5G adoption, 27% cloud computing uptake, and 25 operational data centres nationwide.

**His angle:** The bottleneck is not talent. It is infrastructure and integration. Workers ready to use AI cannot get value from it when the systems beneath them are disconnected spreadsheets and manual approval chains. The enterprise adoption lag is an ops problem, not a training problem.

### 6. Nigeria cut import duties on July 1. Procurement teams have a short window to reprice planned imports.

**Date:** Announced April 14, 2026, effective July 1, 2026

**Hook:** Duties went down on July 1. The companies that act on this in the next few weeks pay less than those that notice it next quarter.

**Context:** Nigeria cut import duties on rice, sugar, palm oil, passenger vehicles, and construction materials, effective July 1, 2026, as part of a cost-of-living package. Oil and gas procurement teams are separately navigating a narrowed VAT exemption list under the Nigeria Tax Act 2025 VAT Modification Order, which now requires a FIRS exemption certificate before clearing equipment at port. The two policy shifts create both an opportunity and a new compliance step, depending on what a company imports.

**His angle:** Policy changes create a procurement window. Companies with a clean vendor database, current item classifications, and a procurement calendar connected to their finance records will adapt in days. Those still reconciling purchase orders by email will find out months later. Policy friction rewards organized procurement.

**Format:** Quick note

**Source:** https://www.africanews.com/2026/04/14/nigeria-slashes-import-duties-to-fight-rising-cost-of-living/
