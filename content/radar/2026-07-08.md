## 1. NCDMB's vendor audit will separate the paper-compliant from the actually ready

**Hook:** Nigeria's local oil and gas participation just hit 61%. In Q3, auditors will
start showing up at vendor facilities. Paper compliance is not going to hold up.

**Context:** At NOG Energy Week 2026 on July 6, NCDMB announced that local participation

**His angle:** Vendors who store their procurement records, inventory counts, and
compliance documents across scattered folders and WhatsApp threads will struggle to answer
auditor questions on short notice. The companies that pass with confidence will be those
whose operations already live in one place. This is not about software. It is about being
able to show your actual business, not a curated document dump prepared the night before.

## 2. Oracle shipped four AI agents for supply chain. One handles supplier qualification.

**Hook:** Oracle released four agentic applications for supply chain management on June 29.
The one to watch for procurement teams: an agent that runs supplier onboarding and
compliance tracking without a person chasing it.

**Context:** The four Fusion Agentic Applications (Inventory Planning Command Center,
Supplier Qualification Workspace, Production Readiness Workspace, and Kanban
Administrative Workspace) run inside Oracle Fusion Cloud SCM. They move from assisting
users to executing routine tasks, and surface decisions where human judgment is needed.
The Supplier Qualification Workspace handles onboarding, credential tracking, and
compliance status continuously.

**His angle:** Nigerian oil and gas procurement teams do supplier qualification manually
today: phone calls, email follow-ups, spreadsheets of certificate expiry dates. An agent
can run that loop continuously and flag problems before they cause a work stoppage. The
real question for operators considering this kind of tool is whether their supplier data
is clean and organized enough to feed it. In most Nigerian operations, it is not. That is
the work to do first, before buying any AI.

**Suggested format:** LinkedIn post

**Source:** https://www.oracle.com/news/announcement/oracle-adds-new-fusion-agentic-applications-to-help-customers-improve-supply-chain-performance-2026-06-29/

## 3. 72% of enterprises have AI agents in production. 60% have no governance for them.

**Hook:** Agentic AI is not a future trend. It is already in production at most large
companies. The risk has shifted from falling behind to deploying without control.

**Context:** A July 2026 report found that 72% of enterprise AI use cases are in
production, but a 60% governance gap remains. Only 21% of organisations have a mature
governance model for their AI agents. 35% admit they could not shut down a rogue AI agent
if one emerged. Gartner predicts more than 40% of agentic AI projects will be cancelled
by 2027, not because of technical failures but because of governance failures.

**His angle:** For clients running or considering AI agents for procurement approvals or
inventory decisions, the practical test is this: do you know exactly what data the agent
touches, what it can decide alone, and how you override it when it is wrong? One
connected system is not just about efficiency. It is about having one place to set the
rules and one place to audit what actually happened.

**Suggested format:** LinkedIn post

**Source:** https://agenticaiinstitute.org/agentic-ai-enterprise-adoption-2026-governance-gap/

## 4. Nigeria's founders are moving from payments to operations software

**Hook:** The next wave of Nigerian tech is not another consumer payments app. Founders
are building B2B software for logistics, supply chain, compliance, and workforce
management. The market is catching up to the problem.

**Context:** Recent Business Day reporting documents a clear shift among Nigerian startup
founders away from consumer fintech toward enterprise software. The drivers: predictable
Naira revenue, lower regulatory risk from CBN, and the fact that software built for
Nigeria's operational realities exports well to other African markets. 103 SaaS companies
now operate in Nigeria with combined revenue of $2.1 billion.

**His angle:** This is the market validating what Bamidele already does. Businesses
managing procurement, inventory, and invoicing across four tools that do not talk to each
other are the exact customers this wave is building for. The people who understand that
pain from the inside (not just from a product roadmap) have something specific and useful
to say right now. Write the post that a software founder would wish they had read before
they started.

global funding slowed. The more telling number: 63 mergers and acquisitions, nearly
double the pace of H1 2025.

**Context:** TechCabal (July 3, 2026) reported that African tech held steady against
global headwinds. Nigeria was the busiest market by deal count. The M&A acceleration

**His angle:** When three procurement tools get acquired into one platform, or when a

quickly. Companies on scattered tools face months of painful migration. The consolidation
wave makes integration hygiene a strategic concern, not just an IT task.

## 6. Oil and gas MRO: digital procurement operators are spending 15 to 30% less

**Hook:** The global oil and gas MRO market is worth $68 billion. Operators who have
connected their maintenance, inventory, and procurement data are spending 15 to 30% less
than those still running reactive maintenance on paper.

**Context:** The 2026 MRO market outlook documents a measurable gap forming between
operators who have integrated IoT condition monitoring, real-time inventory tracking, and
digital e-procurement, and those still operating on manual processes. The market is
growing at 5.7% CAGR through 2034, with digital integration as the primary cost
differentiator between high-cost and low-cost operators.

**His angle:** In Nigeria, most mid-market oil and gas vendors still run MRO through a
combination of phone calls, WhatsApp messages, and paper goods receipt notes (GRNs). The
15 to 30% cost difference is not a global abstraction. It represents what a Lagos-based
vendor leaves on the table each quarter because their maintenance records, stock levels,
and purchase orders do not connect. Consolidating those three into one system is where
the savings start, before any AI is involved.

**Suggested format:** Article

**Source:** https://www.verdantis.com/oil-gas-refinery-mro-optimization/
