## 5. Digital Procurement Africa Summit: Nigerian firms say legacy procurement must go, but integration is the blocker

**Hook:** The first Digital Procurement Africa Summit confirmed that manual procurement processes cost Nigerian companies real money. The hard part is not deciding to change. It is connecting the new tools to each other.

**Context:** At the inaugural Digital Procurement Africa Summit in Lagos (May 2026), procurement leaders agreed legacy processes must be replaced. The most common blockers cited were infrastructure limitations and integration with existing systems. Nigerian companies are adopting digital platforms for better supplier visibility and cost reduction. Separately, Nigerian SMEs are at 81% optimism about the next 12 months, with 100% agreeing digital payments are critical to their future.

**Your angle:** Buying a procurement platform is not procurement digitization. Procurement digitization is connecting the purchase order to the inventory count to the payment approval to the budget line. If those four things still live in four different systems after the new platform goes in, the process is still manual. The summit surfaced the right diagnosis. The right treatment is integration, not replacement.

## 7. Global oil and gas MRO market at $68bn, with e-procurement cutting processing costs by up to 30%

**Hook:** The global MRO market for oil and gas is at $68 billion. The operators cutting costs fastest are the ones who connected their procurement to their maintenance data.

**Context:** The global MRO in oil and gas market was valued at $68.4 billion in 2025 and is projected to reach $112.7 billion by 2034. Operators integrating AI-driven predictive maintenance with e-procurement platforms are documenting 15 to 30 percent reductions in total MRO cost versus reactive maintenance approaches. E-procurement tools that automate purchase orders and approvals are showing up to 30 percent reduction in processing costs.

**Your angle:** The 30 percent cost reduction is not from buying better parts. It is from connecting maintenance data to procurement data, so you know what you need before it fails and you are not paying emergency rates. That connection is the same principle applied to any operations context: when data moves between systems automatically, decisions improve and costs fall. The MRO case is just the most measurable version of it.
